**Curator's take**  
The paper tackles the problem of evaluating factual completeness in long‑form, open‑ended generation. Meta‑AI researchers (Chen et al.) propose a two‑level meta‑rubric framework that first authorises rich, structured criteria and then mechanically compiles them into flat, binary checks that an LLM judge can score reliably. They instantiate this as Gamut, a multimodal benchmark of 1,813 real‑world questions grounded in wearable imagery.

**Key points**

- **Problem & motivation** – Existing factuality metrics focus on precision (fraction of correct claims) and reduce answers to independent boolean checks, ignoring recall and richer answer structure. The authors argue that factual completeness requires a structured representation of what a complete answer should contain.

- **Two‑level meta‑rubric** – A structured meta‑rubric captures the organization, importance, and relations among required facts (e.g., critical vs. valuable items, process order). It is mechanically compiled into a flat checklist of binary, machine‑gradable checks that an LLM judge scores reliably, reconciling expressiveness with low‑variance grading.

- **Gamut benchmark** – Gamut contains 1,813 questions across 10 domains, each paired with evidence‑backed rubrics verified by expert annotators. Questions are generated from real images drawn from CRAG‑MM, reflecting realistic wearable‑assistant scenarios. A text‑only variant is also released.

- **Evaluation results** – Evaluating 14 frontier and open‑weight models shows Gamut is genuinely challenging: the best model, Gemini 3.1 Pro, achieves a Gamut score of only 58.7 %. The benchmark is highly discriminative, rankings align with independent model capabilities, and results are robust to the choice of judge. Removing visual identification in the text‑only variant raises scores by a roughly constant amount across models, indicating the multimodal ranking reflects knowledge completeness rather than perception alone.

- **Why it matters** – The work highlights that factual recall remains far from solved, with large gaps that precision‑based evaluation fails to reveal. By providing a reliable, expressive evaluation framework and a realistic multimodal benchmark, it encourages research toward truly complete, knowledge‑seeking answers.
