**Curator's take**  
The paper tackles the high cost of global attention in long‑context language models. Namgyu Ho, Huzama Ahmad, Woosung Koh, Se‑Young Yun, Tal Schuster, and Cicero Nogueira dos Santos propose Declarative Attention (DA), a zero‑shot protocol that lets a model explicitly declare whether to attend globally, to a focused chunk, or locally, thereby skipping most KV‑cache reads.

**Key points**

- **Problem & motivation** – Transformers attend every preceding token at each decoding step, making long‑context decoding expensive (e.g., Qwen‑3.5‑397B‑A17B must load 15 GB of FP8 KV cache per step for a 1 M‑token context). Empirical studies show attention naturally concentrates on a small subset of tokens, but prior methods still scan the full cache per step (O(N) cost).

- **Declarative Attention (DA)** – DA introduces three attention modes: `<global>` (full context), `<focus>` (a named chunk), and `<local>` (recent output only). The model emits these mode tokens as part of its chain‑of‑thought; the inference engine parses them to build a dynamic attention mask, eliminating most KV‑cache reads.

- **Zero‑shot effectiveness** – Across 15 long‑context tasks, DA on off‑the‑shelf models reduces total attended tokens per response by **45.8 %** on Gemma‑4‑31B and **19.6 %** on Qwen‑3.6‑27B, despite extra decoding steps. Accuracy drops are modest: **1.40 pp** for Gemma‑4‑31B and **2.10 pp** for Qwen‑3.6‑27B, with the gap shrinking as model size increases.

- **Scaling & implementation** – The benefit grows with model size (accuracy gap closes from 4 B to 31 B) and with context length (up to 19 M tokens saved per response). Integrating DA into vLLM with block‑aligned KV cache masking yields up to **55 %** per‑step decode‑time reduction, without kernel changes.

- **Why it matters** – DA offers a new axis of sparse attention that is intrinsic to the model’s reasoning, avoiding per‑step proxy‑score scans and enabling efficient long‑context inference on existing large models.
