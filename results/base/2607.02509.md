**Curator's take**  
ReContext tackles the gap between long‑context access and effective utilization in large language models. The authors from the University of Illinois Urbana‑Champaign propose a training‑free inference technique that recursively replays internally identified evidence before final generation, thereby improving long‑context reasoning without pruning or external memory.

**Key points**  
- **Problem & Motivation** – Long‑context LLMs can ingest entire documents but often ignore evidence already present in the prompt, leading to unreliable reasoning (Yen et al., 2025; Ye et al., 2026; Bei et al., 2026).  
- **Method** – Recursive Evidence Replay (ReContext) uses question‑conditioned internal attention to build an evidence pool, materializes selected tokens into grounded spans, and replays this pool before final answer generation, all while preserving the full original context.  
- **Theoretical Insight** – The authors model the context as an associative memory store, the question as a retrieval cue, attention as cue‑trace association, and replay as trace reactivation, providing a monotonic‑improvement proof that the hidden representation moves toward the answer embedding.  
- **Empirical Scope** – ReContext is evaluated on eight 128K‑token long‑context datasets with Qwen3‑4B, Qwen3‑8B, and Llama3‑8B, achieving the best average rank on all three backbones and improving mean accuracy over Vanilla from 0.24 to 0.30, a 24.6 % relative gain.  
- **Why it matters** – By leveraging internal relevance signals without training or external memory, ReContext offers a lightweight, inference‑time harness that can be applied to existing LLMs to enhance long‑context reasoning performance.
