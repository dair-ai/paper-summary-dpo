**Curator's take**  
The paper tackles the problem of turning long‑horizon LLM agent experience into reusable, verifiable skills.  
The authors from MemTensor and partner universities propose MSCE, a training‑free Memory–Skill Co‑Evolution framework that organizes experience into grounded traces, procedural policies, and environmental cognition, then promotes only evidence‑backed, positive‑gain L2 policies into callable skills.

**Key points**

- **Memory hierarchy** – MSCE splits experience into three levels: L1 trace memory (grounded step‑level evidence), L2 policy memory (recurring procedural patterns), and L3 environmental cognition memory (declarative knowledge about structure and constraints).  
- **Skill promotion criteria** – A policy is promoted to a skill only if it retains supporting evidence, shows positive estimated gain, and remains consistent with its trigger, procedure, and applicability boundary. The resulting skill keeps evidence anchors, verification rules, and a reliability‑driven lifecycle.  
- **Reflection‑weighted value back‑filling** – Sparse terminal feedback is propagated through dense local self‑reflections to produce evidence‑calibrated trace values, which serve as unified signals for retrieval, policy induction, environmental abstraction, skill promotion, and later revision.  
- **Empirical claim** – Experiments on EvoAgentBench and LoCoMo demonstrate that MSCE significantly outperforms state‑of‑the‑art skill‑augmented and memory‑driven agent baselines, showing strong cross‑domain transferability and lifelong‑evolution capabilities.
