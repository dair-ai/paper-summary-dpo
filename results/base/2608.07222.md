**Curator’s take**  
The paper tackles the systematic bias of the widely used Chinchilla scaling law, which treats model size and data as independent. Meta researchers Mathurin Videau, Badr Youbi‑Idrissi, David Lopez‑Paz, and Kartik Ahuja propose the *Skaling* law, a minimal extension that couples the two variables through a single interaction exponent. They show that Skaling cuts mean absolute percentage error (MAPE) by 1.5–3× and enables a 10× cheaper “L‑shape” profiling strategy.

**Key points**

- **Problem & motivation** – Standard additive scaling (Chinchilla) under‑ and over‑estimates loss at grid corners because it assumes zero cross‑derivative between model size \(N\) and training tokens \(D\). Empirical mixed‑derivative estimates (Figure 3) are non‑zero, indicating a coupling that additive laws miss.

- **Skaling law** – Introduces one interaction exponent between \(N\) and \(D\). With this single parameter, Skaling reproduces the negative mixed derivative and the small asymmetric first‑order slopes observed in the data (Section 2). It reduces extrapolation MAPE by 1.5–3× relative to Chinchilla across multiple cross‑validation strategies.

- **Sparse profiling grids** – By sweeping data volume for small models (D‑band) and model size on a fixed small data set (N‑band), the Skaling law achieves predictive accuracy comparable to a full‑grid fit while cutting profiling compute by roughly 10× (Section 3.2).

- **Why it matters** – Skaling provides a more reliable, resource‑efficient framework for allocating compute budgets in next‑generation model training, especially when experimental budgets are constrained.
