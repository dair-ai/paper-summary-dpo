**Curator's take**  
The paper tackles the problem of procedural incoherence in long‑horizon LLM agents. Yuxing Lu, Yicheng Chen, Shanchan Wu, and Sercan Ö. Arık propose the Procedural Graph (PG), an explicit, editable directed graph that encodes procedural knowledge as (procedure, relation, procedure) triplets. They combine online guidance derived from the graph with an offline self‑evolution loop that refines the graph using execution feedback.

**Key points**  
- **Procedural Graph (PG)** – An explicit, editable directed graph where nodes represent tool actions, reasoning steps, or states, and edges encode permissible transitions with textual attributes.  
- **Generative PG Guidance** – During inference the agent localizes its active node, reads the surrounding subgraph, and translates edge attributes into step‑level situational guidance that biases the next action without dictating it.  
- **Self‑evolution loop** – After each batch of training tasks, an LLM refiner contrasts failed and successful trajectories, proposes edits (adding, pruning, or revising nodes/edges), and adopts a candidate graph if it matches or improves held‑out validation performance; rejected candidates are kept as negative constraints.  
- **Why it matters** – The PG keeps procedural knowledge outside model weights, allowing inspection, retrieval, and editing without retraining, and the self‑evolution mechanism can repair flawed expert priors or build effective graphs from a minimal skeleton.
