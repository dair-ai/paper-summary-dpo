**Curator's take**  
The paper tackles whether a language model can serve as a practical corpus‑scale retriever.  
Siddharth Gollapudi et al. build a 0.6 B‑parameter model, B LOCK SEARCH, that matches a dense retriever on small corpora and generalizes up to 10× larger contexts, but collapses near a million tokens due to attention dilution. They propose length‑aware softmax scaling and document‑level sparse attention to recover most of the lost performance.

**Key points**

- **Problem & baseline** – Retrieval is usually vector‑based; in‑context retrieval (ICR) would let a single LM both retrieve and generate. Prior work only examined small or proprietary setups, so the paper studies ICR on million‑token corpora and extreme length generalization.

- **B LOCK SEARCH** – A 0.6 B‑parameter LM that uses block‑sparse attention, randomized per‑document IDs, in‑batch negatives, and an on‑policy auxiliary loss. It matches a same‑backbone dense retriever on small corpora (95.8 % vs. 95.5 % Recall@1 on MS MARCO at ~45k tokens) and keeps non‑trivial accuracy up to 10× its training context.

- **Attention dilution failure** – As corpus size grows, irrelevant documents dominate the softmax denominator, driving the gold document’s normalized attention mass from 0.91 to 0.01. Individual heads still rank the gold document first more often than the model decodes it, but this signal also weakens with N.

- **Mitigation techniques** – Length‑aware softmax scaling (SSMax, scaling pre‑softmax scores with log N) and document‑level sparse attention reduce attention dispersion. These raise million‑token Recall@1 on MS MARCO from 0.2 % to 20.0 % (vs. 20.2 % for the dense baseline) and improve HotpotQA from 78.5 % to 79.5 % at N = 10k.

- **Out‑of‑distribution success** – On the LIMIT benchmark, the same modifications let B LOCK SEARCH achieve 13.5 % Recall@1 at a million‑token corpus, far above the dense baseline’s 2.9 %.

- **Why it matters** – The study shows that corpus‑scale ICR is feasible and can rival dense retrieval, but attention dilution is a fundamental bottleneck that must be addressed for extreme context growth.
