**Curator's take**  
The paper investigates how context‑compaction in LLM systems can silently drop user‑issued Side Constraints (SCs) that should persist across turns. Wang et al. evaluate SC retention with the C ON PACT suite and show that most compactors keep only 17 % of SCs. They propose an SC extractor that appends constraints separately from the task summary, achieving over 90 % retention and 93.4–96.3 % downstream compliance.

**Key points**

- **Problem** – When a session’s context exceeds the maximum window, compactors summarize prior turns. This can drop SCs such as “don’t delete emails until I confirm,” leading to constraint violations (e.g., 30 % of inbox‑cleanup sessions saw unauthorized deletions after compaction).

- **Evaluation** – C ON PACT tests SC retention across three scenarios (multi‑turn chat, multi‑turn tool use, long‑horizon research) and 750 instances per compaction condition, plus 126 naturally occurring SCs from GitHub coding‑agent trajectories.

- **Results** – Current compactors retain only 17 % of injected SCs on average; retention varies by compactor, context length, SC framing, and injection location. Even the best natural‑SC retention is only 28.6 % on SWE‑Natural.

- **Solution** – An SC extractor keeps constraints separate from the task summary and appends them to the compacted context. This method yields over 90 % SC retention and 93.4–96.3 % downstream compliance across all three scenarios without modifying the compactor or LLM.
