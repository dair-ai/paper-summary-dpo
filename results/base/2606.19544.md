**Curator's take**  
The paper tackles the reliability of LLM-as-a-Judge systems, showing that the common exact‑match metric inflates performance. Norman, Rivera, and Hughes evaluate 21 judges from nine providers across MTBench, JudgeBench, and RewardBench, and propose a Minimum Viable Validation Protocol that corrects for chance and highlights bias and consistency issues.

**Key points**  
- **Kappa deflation** – Exact‑match agreement overstates chance‑corrected discrimination by 33–41 pp on MT‑Bench for all 21 judges.  
- **Ranking instability** – Judge rankings shift by up to 14 positions across benchmarks, indicating non‑transferable performance.  
- **Consistency–bias paradox** – Two production‑deployed judges show test–retest reliability > 0.95 while exhibiting severe position bias > 0.10.  
- **Verbosity bias** – Across the cohort, verbosity bias is small (< 0.011) under a single pairwise rubric, far below the 20–40 % variance reported in earlier literature.  
- **Benchmark sharpness** – JudgeBench discriminates 4.5× more sharply than MT‑Bench (60.4 pp vs. 13.5 pp κ spread).  

*Why it matters* – The study reveals that widely used validation practices overestimate judge quality, underscoring the need for chance‑corrected metrics and comprehensive bias audits in LLM evaluation pipelines.
