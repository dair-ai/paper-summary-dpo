**Curator's take**  
The paper tackles Long‑Term Coherence for autonomous agents by introducing MerchantBench, a 365‑day e‑commerce simulation built on 98,843 real product records. Alibaba researchers evaluate eight LLMs under two agent frameworks, showing that even the best configuration reaches only 27.3 % of the mean final net assets of human participants.

**Key points**  
- **Benchmark scope** – MerchantBench simulates seller‑side e‑commerce over 365 days, requiring agents to repeatedly source products, set listings and prices, manage cash flow, and adapt to delayed feedback from upstream suppliers and downstream orders.  
- **Data grounding** – The environment uses 98,843 real e‑commerce product records and generates individual orders that progress through fulfillment and after‑sales stages, creating a persistent, partially observable state.  
- **Evaluation setup** – Eight large language models are tested under two agent frameworks in 48 runs, each run spanning a full simulated year.  
- **Performance gap** – The best LLM configuration attains only 27.3 % of the mean final net assets achieved by human participants, highlighting a substantial gap in Long‑Term Coherence.  
- **Why it matters** – The study demonstrates that current state‑of‑the‑art LLM agents struggle to maintain coherent, goal‑oriented behavior over extended horizons in realistic, dynamic business settings.
