**Curator's take**  
The paper tackles the problem of pinpointing which turns in a multi‑turn tool‑use dialogue actually benefit from training. Salesforce AI Research proposes *Critical‑State RL*, a diagnostic that separates action‑dependent reward variation from downstream noise and then trains only the selected turns with contextual‑bandit updates.  

**Key points**  
- **Problem & motivation** – Multi‑turn tool use rewards are trajectory‑level, so it is unclear which individual model calls should be improved.  
- **Critical‑State diagnostic** – By nested sampling of alternative actions and continuations, the method isolates the reward signal that truly depends on the current action.  
- **Training recipe** – Once a candidate call is deemed “critical,” the policy is updated at that turn using contextual‑bandit training while other turns provide context or reward but no gradient.  
- **Empirical findings** – On the Berkeley Function Calling Leaderboard v4, the diagnostic selects the response after tool availability for missing‑function tasks and the response before missing arguments for missing‑argument tasks. Training these selected turns yields about **14 percentage points** improvement on the missing‑function task, whereas training alternative turns leaves performance flat or worse.  
- **Why it matters** – The approach offers a training‑free way to identify the most impactful turns, potentially reducing data and compute costs for fine‑tuning large language models in tool‑use scenarios.
