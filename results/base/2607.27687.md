**Curator's take**  
The paper tackles the “confidence cliff” problem in autoresearch loops: as more successful changes accumulate, the agent’s pre‑execution judgment of which new modification will improve the metric becomes less reliable. Ji Jiazhen and Ding Shouhong from Tencent propose **Rehearse**, a lightweight skill that adds a pre‑run comparison step and a focused outcome‑memory module to keep selective accuracy high even late in the loop.

**Key points**

- **Confidence cliff** – On the 366‑pair benchmark, a memoryless judge’s selective accuracy drops from 82.8 % at cold start to 56.9 % once three or more successes have accumulated, while its willingness to decide stays high.  
- **Focused outcome memory** – Using a focused store of similar past outcomes, Rehearse raises late selective accuracy to 83.5 % (vs. 80.9 % for the closest‑summary baseline).  
- **Pre‑execution judgment** – On 296 same‑baseline pairs, an LLM judge with candidate rationales but no prior‑attempt history achieves 79.5 % accuracy on pairs where strict consensus returns a verdict.  
- **Efficiency gains** – Across 4,000 budgeted training runs over three loops, Rehearse improves the endpoint under the same training‑run budget on nanochat, image classification, and time‑series forecasting.
