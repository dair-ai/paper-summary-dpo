**Curator’s take**  
The paper asks whether current agent‑native memory systems are ready for production use. Wei Zhou and colleagues from Shanghai Jiao Tong and Tsinghua systematically evaluate 12 memory architectures from a data‑management lens, decomposing them into representation, extraction, retrieval, and maintenance modules and measuring effectiveness, fidelity, robustness, stability, and cost.

**Key points**

- **Scope & methodology** – The study evaluates 12 representative memory systems (e.g., MemoryBank, MemGPT, Mem0, Zep, A‑MEM) across five benchmark workloads covering 11 datasets, using a unified testbed that records time‑overhead traces.  
- **No single winner** – Results show that no architecture dominates all scenarios; composite hybrid systems excel in conversational QA, while graph‑based methods perform best on single‑hop factual recall but falter on temporal reasoning.  
- **Retrieval fidelity drops with time** – Explicit query planning and balanced hybrid search improve contextual relevance, yet retrieval accuracy degrades significantly as the temporal distance between evidence and query increases, highlighting limits of similarity‑based retrieval.  
- **Robustness to updates** – Graph‑based methods handle knowledge updates most reliably; append‑only stores and fact‑extraction plugins struggle with targeted overwrites, leading to stale facts and “hallucinations of the past”.  
- **Long‑horizon stability** – Append‑only stores suffer catastrophic degradation as evidence ages; raw long‑context retrieval still outperforms most memory‑backed approaches for time‑dependent queries, suggesting that standard semantic consolidation can destroy chronological cues.  
- **Operational costs** – Highly structured systems incur orders‑of‑magnitude higher index construction time and query latency than lightweight stores, yet do not consistently deliver proportional accuracy gains.  
- **Why it matters** – By decomposing memory into four core modules and providing fine‑grained ablation studies, the paper offers actionable insights for building cost‑effective, robust, and stable agent‑native memory systems.
