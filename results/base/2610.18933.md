**Curator's take**  
The paper introduces *The Agentic Scientist*, a human‑steerable orchestration framework that chains together coding agents for the full scientific workflow—ideation, parallel sandboxed experiments, self‑verification via rubrics, and manuscript writing. The authors demonstrate the system’s ability to produce a conference‑accepted paper and to autonomously discover methods across several machine‑learning domains.

**Key points**  
- **System design** – The Agentic Scientist decomposes research into task drafting, idea generation, planning, sandboxed experimentation, result aggregation, baseline/ablation construction, post‑hoc analysis, and report writing, all coordinated by a meta‑controller that enforces rubric verification.  
- **Rubric‑based verification** – Unlike earlier versions (AI Scientist‑v1/v2), this system adds rubric‑based self‑verification and CLI agents for broader code edits, enabling more reliable experiment validation and reducing overfitting.  
- **Co‑scientist success** – A development version of the system was used to automate parts of a study on error diffusion, and the resulting paper was accepted as an ALIFE 2026 talk after limited human edits and verification.  
- **Cross‑domain validation** – The framework was applied to hyper‑network LLM adaptation, a meta‑trained evolution strategy that matches or exceeds CMA‑ES on 5 of 7 unseen Brax control tasks, and RL‑based LLM fine‑tuning, demonstrating broad applicability.  
- **autoillustrate** – An agentic figure‑generation pipeline that outperforms PaperBanana on PaperBananaBench (37.5 vs. 25.0 overall score, Zhu et al., 2026a).  
- **Why it matters** – By extending beyond simple hill‑climbing, the system enables fully autonomous scientific discovery and co‑scientist collaboration, potentially accelerating research across diverse computational domains.
