**Curator’s take**  
JAXBench fills a missing gap by providing a TPU‑native benchmark suite for AI‑generated kernel optimization. The authors, a Google/Harvard/UC‑Berkeley/DeepMind team, propose 50 JAX workloads (17 production LLM operators + 33 fused operators) with expert‑tuned Pallas kernels as upper bounds, and evaluate several agent‑driven generation methods on TPU v6e.

**Key points**

- **Scope & design** – 50 JAX workloads: 17 production operators from MaxText (Llama‑3.1, DeepSeek‑V3, Mixtral, Mamba‑2, AlphaFold2) and 33 fused operators translated from KernelBench. All problem sizes are set to saturate TPU v6e MXUs, ensuring compute‑bound workloads.

- **Baselines** – 8 priority kernels ship with hand‑optimized Pallas kernels from Tokamax, block‑size tuned to establish an expert upper‑bound baseline. XLA compilation serves as the generic baseline.

- **Agent evaluation** – Four feedback‑driven methods are tested on Gemini 3 Flash. Conditioning on curated TPU documentation raises per‑sample correctness from 5.8 % to 37.3 % and solves 48 of 50 benchmarks with a 1.28× geomean speedup over XLA. Autocomp’s beam‑search pipeline then yields a 1.36× geomean speedup over XLA, and 1.60× over XLA on the 8 hand‑tuned kernels, recovering most of the 2.08× Tokamax upper bound but trailing on paged and ragged attention operators.

- **Why it matters** – The paper demonstrates that a TPU‑specific benchmark is essential because GPU benchmarks cannot capture TPU‑unique concerns (VMEM/SMEM/HBM hierarchies, software pipelining, block‑shape constraints, Mosaic’s lexicographic grid). JAXBench provides the first contemporary TPU benchmark with production‑scale workloads, expert references, and a reproducible evaluation harness, enabling meaningful progress in autonomous TPU kernel optimization.
