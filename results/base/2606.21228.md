**Curator's take**  
Sakana AI proposes Sakana Fugu, a family of orchestrator language models that dynamically assemble a team of frontier LLM agents to solve user queries. The authors claim that Fugu and its higher‑latency variant Fugu‑Ultra achieve state‑of‑the‑art results on a range of coding, reasoning, and scientific benchmarks, surpassing publicly available models such as Gemini 3.1‑Pro, GPT‑5.5, and Opus 4.8.

**Key points**  
- **Problem & approach** – Frontier LLMs specialize in different domains; Fugu learns to orchestrate a pool of such agents, selecting or composing them per query to amplify overall capability.  
- **Model variants** – Fugu balances speed and quality by routing each input to a single best agent; Fugu‑Ultra composes multiple agents, trading latency for higher answer quality on the hardest problems.  
- **Performance** – Across benchmarks (SWE‑Bench Pro, Terminal Bench, LiveCodeBench, GPQA‑Diamond, Humanity’s Last Exam, CharXiv Reasoning), Fugu and Fugu‑Ultra match or exceed publicly accessible frontier models. For example, on LiveCodeBench Fugu scores 95.5 versus 94.6 for Gemini 3.1‑Pro and 94.3 for GPT‑5.5.  
- **Training & infrastructure** – The system is trained with large‑scale fine‑tuning, evolutionary algorithms, and reinforcement learning, and is designed for production deployment.  
- **Why it matters** – If orchestration can amplify existing models, progress need not rely solely on ever larger training runs, offering a more modular, accessible, and potentially geopolitically balanced path to frontier AI.
