**Curator's take**  
The paper investigates whether “System One” decision models—large language models fine‑tuned to output calibrated probabilities—are superior rerankers compared to plain reranker fine‑tuning on the same backbones. The authors evaluate six Qwen models (0.6 B–27 B) on TREC DL 2019–2023 and five BEIR collections, finding that reranker fine‑tuning consistently outperforms decision fine‑tuning, and that calibration benefits depend on the wording of the question.

**Key points**

- **RQ1 – Ranking performance**  
  From 0.6 B to 9 B, reranker fine‑tuning beats decision fine‑tuning on every backbone by 5 to 29 points nDCG@10. The difference is significant in 48 of 60 reranker‑vs‑decision pairs, never favoring the decision fine‑tune, and holds even on collections not seen during fine‑tuning. At 27 B the two tie; no backbone shows decision fine‑tuning as the better reranker.

- **RQ2 – Calibration**  
  When asked “Does this passage answer the query?”, decision models from 4 B on are better calibrated than the published reranker fine‑tunes. However, with the looser S1Rank question they lose this advantage, while their ranking changes little and untuned models benefit more from the wording.

- **Decision fine‑tuning vs. untuned**  
  Up to 9 B, decision fine‑tuning adds no benefit over the untuned model, staying within –2.5 to +0.7 points of simply asking the untuned model the same question, whereas reranker fine‑tuning adds 6 to 30 points.

- **Why it matters**  
  Calibration alone does not compensate for poorer ranking: up to 9 B, a reranker fine‑tune keeps the better passages at a fixed threshold, whereas decision models cannot. Thus, for reranking tasks, fine‑tuning a dedicated reranker is preferable to relying on decision‑fine‑tuned System One models.
