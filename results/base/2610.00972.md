**Curator's take**  
VeriHarness tackles the problem of verifying long‑horizon LLM agent outputs when no external judge or reference answers are available. Caiqi Zhang et al. from Cambridge and Google Cloud AI Research propose a plug‑and‑play harness that turns the generator’s own model into an agentic verifier, using disagreement resolution and consensus challenging to pick and revise artifacts. The method is evaluated on five workspace benchmarks with Gemini 3.5 Flash and Claude Opus 4.8, achieving the best selection scores and notable gains from evidence‑backed revision.

**Key points**

- **Verification principle** – The authors identify two complementary tasks for same‑model verification: (1) a disagreement resolver that tests competing claims against workspace evidence, and (2) a consensus challenger that seeks evidence to refute shared claims or uncover omitted requirements.

- **General‑purpose harness** – VeriHarness is a training‑free, plug‑and‑play harness that equips the base model with a workspace, evidence tools, and reusable verification skills. It is evaluated on five demanding workspace benchmarks with Gemini 3.5 Flash and Claude Opus 4.8, achieving the highest selection scores among the baselines on every benchmark.

- **Evidence‑backed revision** – Adding revision guided by the harness’s findings improves average performance, yielding gains over a single rollout of **6.2 points** with Gemini 3.5 Flash and **6.4 points** with Claude Opus 4.8 (Table 2).

- **Evolving verification skills** – The harness can self‑improve from failure feedback. On held‑out tasks, skills evolved from an empty library outperform a human‑authored library, and evolving from the human‑authored library adds **6.8 points** on APEX‑Agents and **3.7 points** on SpreadsheetBench 2 over that library (Section 5).

- **Why it matters** – VeriHarness demonstrates a scalable, model‑agnostic approach to agentic verification for long‑horizon tasks, enabling reliable artifact selection and revision without stronger external judges.
