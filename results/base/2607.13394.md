**Curator's take**  
The paper tackles the instability of GFlowNet‑style reinforcement learning for large language model (LLM) post‑training. Liu et al. show that the learned partition function \(Z_{\phi}\) is unnecessary and harmful, and propose GFlowRL, which replaces it with an in‑batch Monte‑Carlo estimate plus two stabilizers. GFlowRL scales stably to dense and sparse (MoE) models, outperforming prior GFlowNet methods on math, code, and adversarial benchmarks.

**Key points**

- **Problem & motivation** – Reward‑maximizing RL (PPO, GRPO, etc.) collapses solution diversity, hurting chain‑of‑thought reasoning. GFlowNets sample trajectories proportionally to reward, but prior work (FlowRL, FOR) relies on a learned partition function \(Z_{\phi}\) that becomes a source of gradient instability at LLM scale.

- **Root cause** – In LLM post‑training the policy is a pretrained 10‑B+ model needing modest updates, while \(Z_{\phi}\) is randomly initialized and must learn a complex quantity from scratch. Empirically, replacing \(Z_{\phi}\) with random noise yields comparable performance, and FlowRL’s gradient norms are orders of magnitude larger than GRPO’s, causing instability.

- **Solution (GFlowRL)** – Remove the learned partition network entirely. Use an in‑batch Monte‑Carlo estimate of \(\log Z\) computed from the rollout group already sampled during GRPO. Add two stabilizers: importance‑sampling correction for rollout/trainer drift, and asymmetric flow‑gap clipping for outlier residuals. This keeps the trajectory‑balance objective while restoring training stability and eliminating distributed overhead.

- **Results (reported)** – At 14 B dense scale, GFlowRL reaches a Codeforces rating of **2048 Elo**, +112 over DeepCoder‑14B, +144 over FlowRL‑14B, and +157 over OpenAI’s o1. On adversarial red‑teaming, GFlowRL attains the highest average ASR@1 on AdvBench (**82.5 %**) and HarmBench (**79.5 %**), surpassing the previous SOTA multi‑turn attacker where FlowRL diverges. Scaling to MoE, GFlowRL reaches **1999 Elo** on Qwen3‑30B‑A3B (3 B active params) and transfers to Qwen3‑235B‑A22B on math.

- **Why it matters** – GFlowRL is the first GFlowNet‑style RL algorithm that scales stably across both dense and sparse (MoE) architectures for large reasoning models, providing a practical, diversity‑preserving alternative to reward‑maximizing RL in modern LLM post‑training pipelines.
