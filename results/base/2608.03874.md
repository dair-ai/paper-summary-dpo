**Curator's take**  
The paper tackles whether large‑language‑model agents can autonomously build and reuse skills from a stream of task interactions. Guan et al. introduce *ContinualSkillBench*, a benchmark of five domains with 100 ordered subtasks each, and evaluate several model‑harness setups to see if sequential execution and an evolving skill library improve performance.

**Key points**

- **Benchmark design** – ContinualSkillBench contains five domains (e.g., Law, Healthcare, Finance) with 100 interconnected subtasks per domain. Tasks are ordered by increasing difficulty and by underlying skill dependencies, so downstream objectives implicitly rely on skills practiced earlier.

- **Sequential execution benefit** – Across 15 model‑domain combinations, sequential execution improves performance in 14 cases. The improvement varies substantially across models and domains.

- **Skill maintenance vs. pure ICL** – In an ablation over two models and three domains, retaining context and feedback without an explicit skill library yields nearly the same mean performance as maintaining an explicit skill repository. Thus, much of the gain comes from context and feedback rather than skill consolidation.

- **Selective skill advantages** – Explicit skills provide selective benefits for tasks that require reusable procedures or precise outputs, whereas full‑context in‑context learning is often stronger on open‑ended tasks.

- **Skill accumulation quality** – The weaker model (GPT‑4o) accumulates more skills but reuses them less often, indicating difficulty in consolidating task‑specific experience into reusable procedures. Larger skill repositories do not guarantee effective learning.

- **Why it matters** – The study shows that current in‑context skill evolution mechanisms can support continual adaptation, but they still struggle to consistently consolidate experience into robust and transferable skills.
