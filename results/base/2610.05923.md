**Curator's take**  
VERA tackles the lack of precise, verifiable long‑horizon environments for agentic AI. The authors from NVIDIA, UC‑Santa Cruz, UIUC, Tsinghua, and others build a scalable pipeline that turns benchmark trajectories into sandboxed, rubric‑scored tasks and co‑evolves an LLM and its harness using verified evidence. They release 9,000+ such environments and show that a 9B model co‑evolved with VERA outperforms single‑axis baselines by 10.3–13.0 points, while a 27B model reaches 71.6 on AutoCoWorkBench and 80.7 on AutoMedBench.

**Key points**  
- **Problem & approach** – Long‑horizon agentic work needs environments that are resumable, verifiable, and provide stage‑level evidence. VERA auto‑generates rubrics, executable checks, and an agent judge from existing trajectories, creating restartable sandboxes that only pass an admission loop if they are fully verifiable.  
- **Co‑evolution loop** – VERA alternates between training the model with rubric rewards (checked by a Reward Verifier) and editing the harness (skills added/merged/removed, verified by a Harness Verifier). Attribution reads stage‑wise scores and traces to produce model and harness reports, ensuring each update is justified by evidence.  
- **Scale & results** – The open‑source corpus contains 9,000+ long‑horizon verifiable environments. At 9B, the co‑evolved agent beats the strongest single‑axis baseline by 10.3 points on AutoCoWorkBench and 13.0 on AutoMedBench. At 27B, it scores 71.6 on AutoCoWorkBench (surpassing Claude Opus 4.8) and 80.7 on AutoMedBench (within 1.2 points of the best).  
- **Why it matters** – By providing precise, verifiable evidence at every step, VERA enables stable training and clear attribution of gains, addressing a key bottleneck in long‑horizon agentic AI and opening the door to more reliable self‑improvement cycles.
