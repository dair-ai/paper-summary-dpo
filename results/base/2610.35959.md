**Curator's take**  
The paper tackles the problem of reducing inference cost in long‑horizon agentic tasks by letting a high‑capability model hand off to a cheaper, efficient model at the right moment. Junlong Li et al. propose AgentBaton, a lightweight detector that learns when to trigger such a handoff based on patterns in the agent’s execution history.

**Key points**  
- **Problem & motivation** – Long‑horizon agentic tasks often rely on expensive cloud models, incurring high API costs. Efficient models are cheaper but less accurate. The authors ask whether a capable model can hand off to an efficient one during a task to save cost without sacrificing accuracy.  
- **Handoff direction** – Experiments on 11 model pairs and two benchmarks (Terminal Bench 2.1 and DeepSWE v1.1) show that *capable‑to‑efficient* handoffs preserve or even improve accuracy while cutting cost, whereas *efficient‑to‑capable* handoffs generally underperform capable‑only execution and offer little or no cost benefit.  
- **Context transfer** – Two methods are compared: (1) in‑context transfer (full history pasted into the new model’s prompt) and (2) logfile transfer (structured execution log that the new model can query). The preferred method varies by model pair; no single strategy dominates across all cases.  
- **AgentBaton** – A learned detector that observes the capable model’s progress after each turn and predicts a suitable handoff point. Once triggered, the efficient model takes over until task completion.  
- **Results** – Across the evaluated pairs, AgentBaton reduces API costs by **65.9 % on average** while largely preserving task accuracy, with accuracy gains of up to **8.8 percentage points** over capable‑only execution. It also extends the empirical accuracy–cost Pareto frontier across different reasoning‑effort settings.  
- **Why it matters** – The study demonstrates that a single, well‑timed handoff can achieve substantial cost savings in agentic systems, suggesting a practical strategy for deploying mixed‑capability models in real‑world applications.
