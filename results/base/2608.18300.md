**Curator's take**  
Netflix engineers study how to treat an LLM‑as‑a‑Judge as a lifelong agent that must be built, trained, deployed, and monitored. They propose a four‑phase lifecycle—Birth, Training, Deployment, and Monitoring—along with a new Reasoning‑Aligned Rubric Tuning (RART) method to keep the judge aligned with human judgment while it evaluates recommendation explanations at scale.

**Key points**

- **Birth** – The team defines evaluation criteria and builds curated benchmark datasets with human labels and rationales to initialize the judge.  
- **Training** – Reasoning‑Aligned Rubric Tuning (RART) refines the judge’s rubric by using a meta‑judge over the judge’s reasoning as the learning signal, with an ablation that isolates the contribution of reasoning alignment.  
- **Deployment** – One judge serves two online roles: a quality gate that filters explanations and a reflective generation critic that revises explanations in a self‑reflection loop.  
- **Monitoring** – Continuous Human‑in‑the‑Loop (HITL) alignment detects drift and triggers re‑tuning behind a human review gate.  
- **Why it matters** – The lifecycle framework and RART enable Netflix to evaluate hundreds of thousands of recommendation explanations per week at scale, ensuring they remain accurate, item‑specific, and free of sensitive or offensive content.
