**Curator's take**  
This paper introduces *proactive thought partners*, AI agents that offer higher‑level, personalized cognitive support during writing without user prompts. The authors built a technology probe that lets writers configure each partner’s role and timing, then studied 16 users over a week to see how they customize and engage with such agents.

**Key points**  
- **Design concept** – Proactive thought partners sit in the proactive‑cognitive quadrant of intelligent writing assistants, offering higher‑level support (ideation, reflection, revision) rather than just local text suggestions.  
- **Probe mechanics** – Users create partners by specifying a role (type of cognitive support) and timing conditions; partners then initiate suggestions at appropriate moments, which users can dismiss, use, or apply directly.  
- **User behavior** – Participants treated configuration as prospective planning, tailoring partners to anticipated needs and likely difficulties. During writing they often ignored interventions to preserve flow, but engaged with suggestions that aided idea generation and self‑monitoring.  
- **Representation matters** – Lightweight visual cues and nondirective rhetorical framing made proactive support feel less intrusive, enhancing user acceptance.  
- **Implications** – Design should focus on customization, timing, engagement, and representation to create effective proactive writing assistants.
