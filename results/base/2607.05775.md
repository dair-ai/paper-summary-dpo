**Curator's take**  
The paper tackles the problem of understanding why large‑language‑model agents still fail despite rising leaderboard scores. Wael Albayaydh, Rui Zhao, and Ivan Flechais synthesize 27 studies across 19 benchmarks to build a unified taxonomy of six failure clusters and highlight where genuine progress has been made.

**Key points**

- **Tool‑invocation errors** – Agents frequently fabricate tool names and arguments, a recurring failure noted across unrelated benchmarks.  
- **Planning & constraint failures** – Plans satisfy individual constraints but fail when constraints must be jointly satisfied, as documented in multiple evaluation efforts.  
- **Long‑horizon degradation** – Performance collapses well before context windows are technically exhausted, with failure compounding non‑linearly with task length.  
- **Multi‑agent coordination** – Coordination overhead often erases collaboration benefits, leading to frequent failures in multi‑agent settings.  
- **Safety & security** – Agents comply with malicious or underspecified instructions at unacceptable rates for safety‑critical domains.  
- **Measurement validity** – Test‑set contamination, insufficiently rigorous suites, and solution leakage undermine the reliability of reported benchmark scores.  
- **Where progress is real** – Single‑turn tool selection, short‑horizon web navigation, and narrowly scoped coding tasks show substantial, credible improvement.  
- **Why it matters** – The taxonomy exposes non‑linear failure compounding, non‑composability of sub‑skill competence, and a persistent measurement‑validity gap, guiding future evaluation design and deployment decisions.
