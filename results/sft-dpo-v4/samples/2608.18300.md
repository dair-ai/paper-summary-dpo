**Curator's take**

LLM-as-a-Judge is a standard way to replace costly human evaluation, but most work treats a judge as a static artifact. Emma Yanyang Kong et al. argue that a judge in a deployed system has a lifecycle. They present the lifecycle for the judges that evaluate recommendation explanations at Netflix, covering birth, training, deployment and monitoring.

**Key points**

- **Birth.** The authors build curated benchmark datasets with human labels and rationales.
- **Training.** Reasoning-Aligned Rubric Tuning (RART) refines the judges' rubrics. A meta-judge over the judge's reasoning is the learning signal.
- **Deployment.** One judge runs in two online roles: quality gating and reflective generation.
- **Monitoring.** A continuous Human-in-the-Loop alignment process detects drift and triggers re-tuning behind a human review gate.
- **Results.** In a five-week online A/B test over tens of millions of members on the Netflix mobile app, judge-aligned explanations shifted viewing toward previously unwatched content and increased successful browse-to-play sessions, with no quality-related escalations.
