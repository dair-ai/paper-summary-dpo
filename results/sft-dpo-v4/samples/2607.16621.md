**Curator's take**

Long-horizon LLM agents need memory to keep coherence and reuse experience, but existing systems store traces as passive context instead of turning them into executable knowledge. Skills are more actionable, yet raw traces contain failures and artifacts, and sparse terminal feedback makes credit assignment hard. Bo Tang et al. propose MSCE, a training-free Memory-Skill Co-Evolution framework that organizes experience into grounded step traces, reusable procedural policies and declarative environmental cognition. It promotes only evidence-backed, positive-gain policies into callable skills and uses reflection-weighted value backfilling to propagate sparse terminal feedback through dense self-references.

**Key points**

- **Three memory levels.** L1 trace memory stores grounded step evidence, L2 policy memory induces recurring procedural patterns and L3 environmental cognition abstracts declarative knowledge.
- **Skill promotion.** A policy becomes a skill only if it retains evidence, shows positive estimated gain and stays consistent with its trigger and applicability boundary.
- **Reflection-weighted value backfilling.** Sparse terminal feedback propagates through local self-references to produce evidence-calibrated trace values.
- **Results.** On EvoAgentBench and LoCoMo, MSCE significantly outperforms state-of-the-art skill-augmented and memory-driven baselines, with strong cross-domain transfer and lifelong-evolution gains.
- **Why it matters.** Skills that keep evidence links and reliability estimates are less likely to be invoked in inappropriate contexts.
