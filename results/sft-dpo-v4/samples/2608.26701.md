**Curator's take**

Co-Scientist is a Gemini-based multi-agent system that partners with scientists across hypothesis generation, experimentation and manuscript writing. Previous versions focused on in silico hypothesis generation, but this paper extends the system into an execution-grounded research partner. The authors validate it across materials science, biology and computer science, producing experimentally validated results with real-world significance.

**Key points**

- **Materials science.** Co-Scientist designed a non-hazardous precursor route for MXenes. The top-ranked recipe, executed by human experts, yielded 2D layered structures with key structural similarities to Ti3 C2 T_x MXene, though further experiments are needed to confirm the atomic structure. The system also grew monolayer MoS2, MoSe2 and WS2 in a single attempt, tailoring recipes to laboratory constraints in minutes.
- **Biology.** Co-Scientist built a system to predict emergent swarming phenotypes of engineered E. coli across IPTG concentration gradients from sparse imaging data. The predictions largely matched unpublished wet-lab morphological measurements, suggesting a potential to reduce experimental screening cycles.
- **Computer science.** The system autonomously discovered an inference-time scaling architecture that outperformed six frontier models on HealthBench Hard and Professional, while reducing potential clinical harm under blinded physician evaluation.
- **Paper writing.** A double-blind study with 30 domain experts across 450 reviews found that Co-Scientist's reliability modules reduced hallucination and plagiarism compared to unconstrained baseline systems.
- **Why it matters.** The work shows a generalizable research framework that orchestrates discovery across diverse experimental surfaces while keeping experimental verifiability throughout the workflow.
