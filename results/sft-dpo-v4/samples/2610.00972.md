**Curator's take**

Long-horizon agents produce reports, spreadsheets and other artifacts that are hard to verify without reference answers. Caiqi Zhang et al. study how to strengthen verification with a fixed base model, without access to grading rubrics at test time. They propose VeriHarness, which turns the generator's own model into an agentic verifier by giving it a workspace, evidence tools and reusable verification skills.

**Key points**

- **Disagreement vs. consensus.** A case study on APEXAgents shows that 34% of agreed values are wrong, while 66% are correct. Disputed claims often contain a correct candidate, but the most frequent answer is wrong 47% of the time.
- **Disagreement resolver.** It tests competing claims against source files, data and task constraints.
- **Consensus challenger.** It searches for evidence that could refute consensus values or reveal omitted requirements.
- **Benchmarks.** The authors evaluate on five workspace benchmarks with Gemini 3.5 Flash and Claude Opus 4.8 as both generator and verifier.
- **Results.** VeriHarness achieves the highest selection score among the evaluated baselines on every benchmark with both models. Evidence-backed revision improves average performance, with gains over a single rollout of 6.2 points with Gemini 3.5 Flash and 6.4 points with Claude Opus 4.8.
- **Evolving skills.** On held-out tasks, skills evolved from an empty library outperform the human-authored library, and evolving from the human-authored library adds 6.8 points on APEX-Agents and 3.7 on SpreadsheetBench 2.
- **Why it matters.** The authors release the full pool of about 26,000 rollouts, produced at a cost of over $100,000, to support future research on agentic verification.
