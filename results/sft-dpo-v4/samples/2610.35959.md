**Curator's take**

Long-horizon agentic tasks usually rely on expensive capable models from cloud providers, which can be costly. Cheaper efficient models exist but are less accurate. Junlong Li et al. ask whether capable and efficient models can collaborate within a single task to cut cost while keeping accuracy. They study model handoffs, where one model takes over from another, and focus on handoff direction and context transfer.

**Key points**

- **Efficient-to-capable handoffs.** These generally underperform capable-only execution, sometimes at higher cost, even with logfile transfer.
- **Capable-to-efficient handoffs.** At suitable stages of task progress, these largely preserve or improve accuracy while cutting cost. The best context transfer method varies across models.
- **AgentBaton.** A lightweight detector that learns when to hand off from recurring patterns in task progress. The capable model starts, AgentBaton predicts a handoff point, and the efficient model takes over.
- **Results.** Across 11 model pairs and two benchmarks, AgentBaton cuts API costs by 65.9% on average while largely preserving accuracy, with gains of up to 8.8 percentage points over capable-only execution.
- **Why it matters.** A one-time, well-timed handoff strategy is enough for cost-effective agent collaboration.
