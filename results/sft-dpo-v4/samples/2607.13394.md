**Curator's take**

Reward-maximizing RL collapses to dominant modes, which hurts chain-of-thought reasoning. Generative Flow Networks (GFlowNets) match reward distributions instead, but scaling them to modern LLM post-training is hard. The learned partition function, Zϕ, is a source of gradient instability and engineering overhead. Xiaodong Liu et al. propose GFlowRL, which replaces Zϕ with an in-batch Monte Carlo estimate from the rollout group, and adds two stabilizers.

**Key points**

- **Partition function.** A learned Zϕ is noisy and adds optimizer and synchronization overhead. Replacing it with random noise yields comparable performance.
- **GFlowRL.** The in-batch estimate keeps gradient norms at the same scale as GRPO, removes the auxiliary network, and keeps the reward-distribution-matching objective.
- **Stabilizers.** Importance-sampling correction for rollout/trainer drift and asymmetric flow-gap clipping for outlier residuals.
- **Results.** At 14B dense scale, GFlowRL reaches 2048 Codeforces Elo, exceeding DeepCoder-14B by 112, FlowRL-14B by 144, and OpenAI's o1 by 157. On AdvBench and HarmBench, it attains the highest average ASR@1, 82.5% and 79.5%, beating the previous SOTA multi-turn attacker where FlowRL diverges.
- **MoE.** The recipe transfers to MoE up to 235B parameters, where FlowRL fails to converge.
- **Why it matters.** GFlowRL is the first GFlowNet-style RL algorithm to scale stably across both dense and sparse architectures for large reasoning models.
