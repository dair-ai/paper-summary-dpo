**Curator's take**

Agents often rely on external skill libraries to solve complex tasks, but building a comprehensive library is expensive. In more realistic settings, users only give task descriptions and feedback through a sequence of tasks. It is unclear whether agents can evolve their own skills from these interactions. Tianyi Guan et al. introduce ContinualSkillBench, a benchmark that tests skill evolution over long, heterogeneous task streams.

**Key points**

- **Five domains.** Each domain has 100 interconnected subtasks anchored to three core skills.
- **Ordered by difficulty.** Tasks are presented in a logical progression, so downstream objectives rely on skills practiced earlier.
- **Sequential execution helps.** It improves performance in 14 of 15 model-domain combinations.
- **Skill maintenance vs. context.** Across two models and three domains, retaining context and feedback without skill maintenance yields nearly the same mean performance as maintaining an explicit skill library.
- **Selective benefits.** Explicit skills help tasks that need reusable procedures or precise outputs, while full-context ICL is stronger on open-ended tasks.
- **Weaker models accumulate more skills.** GPT-4o builds a larger but less frequently reused repository.
- **Why it matters.** Current agents can adapt through sequential interaction but still struggle to consolidate experience into transferable skills.
