**Curator's take**

LLM agents are usually tested on bounded tasks with clear success criteria, but real deployments often need long-term coherence: keeping purposeful behavior over extended horizons while adapting to evidence that arrives at heterogeneous delays. Seller-side e-commerce fits this test, with recurrent decisions over sourcing, listing, pricing, cash flow and feedback adaptation. Qiming Shi et al. introduce MerchantBench, a 365-day order-level simulation grounded in 98,843 real product records and 26 tools for agent interaction.

**Key points**

- **Persistent environment.** Upstream supplier events are observable promptly, while downstream order outcomes arrive with delay, so agents must revisit earlier decisions.
- **Changing opportunity set.** Demand trajectories shift over the year, so promising products emerge and existing choices lose value.
- **Results.** Eight LLMs under two agent frameworks were run 48 times, each spanning 365 simulated days. The best configuration reached only 27.3% of the mean final net assets achieved by human participants.
- **Why it matters.** The gap shows that even state-of-the-art agents still struggle with long-term coherence in realistic settings.
