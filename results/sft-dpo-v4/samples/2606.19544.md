**Curator's take**

LLM-as-a-Judge has become the dominant way to evaluate language models, but judge validation still relies on exact-match agreement, a metric that does not correct for chance and overstates discriminative ability. Justin D. Norman et al. present the largest systematic evaluation of LLM-as-a-Judge to date, covering 21 judges from nine providers across MTBench, JudgeBench and RewardBench. They evaluate under agreement, consistency and bias audit protocols over 118 runs and about 541,000 judgments.

**Key points**

- **Kappa deflation.** Raw agreement overstates chance-corrected discrimination by 33 to 41 percentage points on MT-Bench across all 21 models.
- **Non-transferable rankings.** Judge rankings shift by up to 14 positions across benchmarks.
- **Consistency-bias paradox.** High test-retest reliability (above 0.95) coexists with severe position bias (above 0.10) in two production-deployed judges.
- **Low verbosity bias.** All 21 models register less than 0.011 under a single pairwise rubric, compared to 20 to 40 percent variance reported in 2023 literature.
- **JudgeBench is sharper.** JudgeBench discriminates 4.5 times more sharply than MT-Bench, with a 60.4 percentage point versus 13.5 percentage point kappa spread.
- **Why it matters.** Exact-match agreement overstates judge discriminative ability, so judge validation needs chance-corrected metrics.
