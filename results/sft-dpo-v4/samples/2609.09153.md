**Curator's take**

LLM agents that plan over long horizons often act through unconstrained generation over a growing log of prior actions. As trajectories lengthen, agents can lose track of their objectives, invoke tools out of order and repeat unproductive actions. Yuxing Lu et al. propose the Procedural Graph, an explicit and editable directed graph of procedural knowledge. Nodes abstract tool actions, reasoning steps and states, and edges encode permissible transitions with textual attributes. A guidance model reads the surrounding subgraph at each step and biases the next action without dictating it. An LLM refiner edits the graph after each batch of training tasks, adopting changes that match or improve held-out validation performance.

**Key points**

- **Explicit structure.** The graph keeps procedural knowledge outside the model weights, where it can be inspected, retrieved and edited without retraining.
- **Situational guidance.** The guidance model translates edge attributes into step-level guidance at each decision step.
- **Self-evolution.** An LLM refiner proposes edits after each batch of training tasks. A candidate graph is adopted if it matches or improves validation performance.
- **Results.** Across multiple datasets, task types and LLMs, the Procedural Graph consistently outperforms memory-based baselines. Self-evolution from scratch produces graphs that match or surpass hand-designed ones, and the loop can repair flawed expert priors.
- **Why it matters.** Agents can keep track of their objectives and avoid invalid behavior while preserving reasoning freedom.
