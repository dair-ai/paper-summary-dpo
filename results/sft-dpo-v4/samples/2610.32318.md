**Curator's take**

System One models answer a question with a calibrated probability instead of generated text. Within weeks of the first release, Jev was reported as a competitive reranker, and other open models followed. The reports compare Jev with rerankers built on other models, so they cannot separate the effect of decision fine-tuning from the underlying language model. Dinzinger et al. compare decision fine-tuning with plain reranker fine-tuning of the same backbone on six Qwen backbones from 0.6B to 27B parameters and ten TREC DL and BEIR collections. They find that decision fine-tuning never makes the better reranker.

**Key points**

- **Reranker fine-tuning wins.** From 0.6B to 9B, reranker fine-tuning beats decision fine-tuning on every backbone by 5 to 29 points nDCG@10. The difference is significant on 48 of the 60 pairs of reranker fine-tune and collection, never significantly in favour of the decision fine-tune. At 27B the two tie.
- **Decision fine-tuning adds nothing over the untuned model.** Up to 9B, it stays within -2.5 and +0.7 points of simply asking the untuned model the same question, while reranker finetuning adds 6 to 30 points.
- **Calibration depends on the question.** Asked whether a passage answers the query, decision models from 4B on are better calibrated than the published reranker finetunes. Asked S1Rank's question, they lose this advantage while their ranking hardly moves.
- **Calibration does not make up for a worse ranking.** Up to 9B, a reranker fine-tune keeps the better passages at a fixed threshold.
- **Why it matters.** If you need a reranker, fine-tune a reranker.
