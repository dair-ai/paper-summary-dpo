**Curator's take**

Modern coding agents can write code, launch experiments and analyze results, but fully autonomous research agents often get stuck in myopic hill-climbing. Robert Tjarko Lange et al. introduce The Agentic Scientist, an end-to-end pipeline that lets human researchers run computational research alongside coding agents. The system breaks research into drafting, idea generation, planning, sandboxed experimentation, result aggregation, baseline and ablation construction, post-hoc analysis and report writing, all powered by headless coding agents and agentic self-verification.

**Key points**

- **Co-scientist paper.** A development version of the system was used to submit a single paper, which was accepted for publication at ALIFE 2026 after limited human edits and verification.
- **Cross-domain results.** The system was applied to hypernetwork-based inductive bias for rapid Transformer adaptation and a meta-trained evolution strategy that matches or exceeds CMA-ES on 5 of 7 unseen Brax control tasks.
- **Rubric verification.** Each step in the pipeline is verified against a rubric, and a meta-controller manages parallel research agents.
- **autoillustrate.** The figure generation tool outperforms PaperBanana on PaperBananaBench, scoring 37.5 against 25.0.
- **Why it matters.** The authors argue that reliable scientific autonomy depends on both base model capabilities and the cross-agent orchestration layer.
