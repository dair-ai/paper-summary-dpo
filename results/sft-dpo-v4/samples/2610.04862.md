**Curator's take**

Long-horizon problem solving and scientific research need computation to accumulate across attempts. Partial solutions and failed experiments can become useful later, but most inference-time work is organized around individual trajectories. Vedant Shah et al. propose compounding inference, where intermediate work persists and can be built on. Their system, GitSwarm, uses a shared, branchable Git repository as persistent memory. Homogeneous agents work asynchronously, inspect previous commits, and decide whether to explore, verify, refine or combine existing work.

**Key points**

- **All 30 IMO problems.** On IMOProofBench-Advanced, GitSwarm solves all 30 problems in one run using GPT-5.5, with slightly under one million output tokens.
- **ProgramBench.** With GPT-5.5 workers, it scores 79.39% mean, compared with 65.12% for Forced Codex at a comparable compute budget.
- **Neural architecture research.** On Residual Matrix Transformer, two configurations reach held-out losses of 2.890 and 2.894, against 3.147 for the reference, with the same 1.3B-token training budget.
- **Accumulation.** Across ProgramBench runs, 94.7% of contributions are subsequently referenced, and the selected solution's ancestry covers 81.6% to 92.6% of the contribution graph on average.
- **Why it matters.** Persistent memory lets later inference reuse earlier work, so computation can accumulate across otherwise independent episodes.
