**Curator's take**

Vision-language models are trained on billions of images, but do they share our ability to communicate visually? Alexa Tartaglini et al. study how VLMs absorb visual cues and use visualizations to talk to each other. They test whether simple symbols steer VLM behavior on unrelated tasks, how models generate and interpret visualizations of abstract concepts, and how model and human interpretations align.

**Key points**

- **Steering by symbols.** A red heart can strongly steer VLMs toward recursive solutions on coding problems. The steering is largely implicit, as models' direct interpretations diverge from their observed behavior.
- **Generation-interpretation gap.** Models send shape primitives that humans find intuitive, but these cues often fail to steer other models.
- **Creative communication.** In games about abstract concepts and stories, models use visuals to transmit meaning, though some communications remain non-intuitive to humans.
- **Partial alignment.** The visualizations that best steer models or emerge in multi-turn games are often less legible to humans.
- **Why it matters.** VLMs can communicate richly and creatively, but their visual meaning diverges from human conventions in surprising ways.
