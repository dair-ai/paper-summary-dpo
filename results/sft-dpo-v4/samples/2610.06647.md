**Curator's take**

Reinforcement learning pushes large language models forward, but its memory demands keep it out of reach for many researchers. Shaokun Zhang et al. propose LoGRA, which keeps learning signals in low-rank gradient sketches. The sketches support both model updates and policy synchronization, and predicted-KL step control keeps updates from disrupting learning.

**Key points**

- **Memory savings.** LoGRA cuts average training memory by 21.8% at 1.5B and 45.7% at 7B, without hurting performance.
- **Large models.** It trains a 27B model for over 1,100 steps on a single eight-GPU node, where dense Adam runs out of memory.
- **Predicted-KL control.** The method estimates policy changes before each update and scales it to stay within a KL budget.
- **Why it matters.** Lower memory and communication costs make RL post-training practical for more researchers.
