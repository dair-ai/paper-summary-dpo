**Curator's take**

Neural scaling laws predict how loss falls as model size and training data grow, but the standard Chinchilla law adds the two effects independently. That assumption makes it under- and overestimate loss at the edges of the training grid. Mathurin Videau et al. add a single coupling exponent between model size and data volume, calling the result the Skaling law. It keeps the simple form of Chinchilla while restoring the interaction that Kaplan's original law had.

**Key points**

- **Boundary bias.** The Chinchilla law is accurate in the grid interior but develops large, oppositely-signed errors toward the corners, where model size and data are most imbalanced.
- **Mixed derivative.** Any additive law predicts a zero mixed derivative. The authors estimate a non-zero mixed derivative across the whole grid, with a negative sign: scaling size and data together lowers loss more than scaling either alone.
- **MAPE advantage.** Across multiple cross-validation strategies, Skaling reduces extrapolation MAPE by 1.5 to 3 times relative to Chinchilla.
- **Sparse profiling.** Using a sparse L-shaped grid that sweeps data for small models and size on a small fixed data set, Skaling matches the accuracy of Chinchilla fitted on the full grid while using up to 10 times less compute.
- **Why it matters.** Reliable predictions from small-scale experiments let teams allocate compute budgets more robustly and efficiently.
