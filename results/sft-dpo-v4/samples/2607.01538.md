**Curator's take**

Retrieval is usually done with vector-based methods, but language models could do it by generating the document ID directly from the context. Prior work has tested this only on proprietary systems or on small reranking sets, so it is unclear whether LMs can retrieve from large corpora. Siddharth Gollapudi et al. study in-context retrieval on million-token corpora and on sizes far beyond training. They introduce BLOCK SEARCH, a 0.6B LM retriever, and then add length-aware softmax adjustments and document-level sparse attention to control attention as the corpus grows.

**Key points**

- **BLOCK SEARCH.** It matches a same-backbone dense retriever on small corpora, 95.8% vs. 95.5% Recall@1 on MS MARCO at about 45k tokens, and generalizes up to 10x beyond its training context.
- **Attention dilution.** As the corpus grows, irrelevant documents dominate the softmax denominator and the gold document's share falls from 0.91 to 0.01 at L19, while the output magnitude barely changes.
- **Head signal.** Individual heads still rank the gold document first more often than the model decodes it, up to 10k documents, but this signal also weakens with corpus size.
- **Softmax scaling.** Scaling pre-softmax scores with log N (SSMax) improves Recall@1 at million-token context from 0.2% to 20.0%, close to the dense baseline's 20.2%.
- **LIMIT benchmark.** At a million-token corpus, Recall@1 reaches 13.5% versus 2.9% for the dense baseline.
- **Why it matters.** Attention dilution is the main bottleneck for retrieval at million-token scale, and controlling attention is a new challenge for scalable in-context retrieval.
