**Curator's take**

Autoresearch improves code by proposing changes, running full training jobs and keeping the ones that improve the metric. The loop's efficiency depends on deciding before a training run whether a modification will work. Ji Jiazhen et al. study how that pre-execution judgment changes as an autoresearch trajectory progresses, and propose Rehearse, a lightweight skill that proposes several ideas, compares them before execution and remembers only the most similar past outcomes.

**Key points**

- **Helpfulness falls.** In public AutoSOTA logs, helpful modifications fall from 70% in iterations 1-2 to 43% by iteration 6+, and their mean gain shrinks from 3.6% to 0.3%.
- **Judgment degrades.** On 366 same-baseline pairs, a judge with no memory has selective accuracy 82.8% at cold start but only 56.9% once three or more successes have accumulated, while coverage rises from 76% to 85%.
- **Focused memory helps.** On the single-turn benchmark, focused retrieval gives 83.5% selective accuracy, versus 80.9% for the closest summary baseline.
- **Rehearse improves endpoints.** Across 4,000 budgeted training runs over three loops, Rehearse improves the endpoint under the same training-run budget on nanochat, image classification and time-series forecasting.
- **Why it matters.** As useful changes become rarer, the agent also becomes less reliable at judging which remaining changes are worth a run. Rehearse keeps that judgment reliable.
