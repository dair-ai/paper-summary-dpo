**Curator's take**

A language-model agent is defined by its model and its harness, the program that organizes model calls and tool use. Adapting the harness can improve performance without changing model parameters, but good revisions often need repeated testing and may not transfer to other tasks. Alvin Zhang et al. propose harness learning, which trains a proposer model to revise a harness from execution feedback. The proposer is trained with reinforcement learning, using the performance of the revised harness as the reward. At test time the proposer refines the harness from successive executions, without updating model parameters.

**Key points**

- **Training setup.** The proposer edits harness code. It receives the task description, the current harness and an execution report, and outputs a code change. The reward is the task reward of the revised harness.
- **Reasoning Gym.** Training improves revision quality on task families excluded from both supervised and reinforcement learning. In single-step revision the trained 4B proposer outperforms the 35B teacher on average.
- **Multi-hop QA.** A proposer trained on HotpotQA transfers to MuSiQue and 2WikiMultihopQA. Trained proposers can keep improving harnesses over successive rounds of test-time adaptation.
- **Seed-harness inputs.** Even a proposer trained only on seed-harness inputs continues improving revised harnesses, suggesting generalization across adaptation contexts and tasks.
- **Why it matters.** Harness learning learns a reusable revision procedure that transfers to unseen tasks without updating model parameters at test time.
