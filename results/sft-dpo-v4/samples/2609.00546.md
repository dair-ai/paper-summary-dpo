**Curator's take**

Long-lived AI agents may replace their models, harnesses, interaction surfaces and hosts while keeping the same identity, memory and executable body. Existing systems have persistent memory and resumable sessions, but those mechanisms do not define the agent boundary. Zhenyu Zhao proposes a runtime-independent architecture that separates a continuity-bearing substrate (I, M, B) from replaceable execution and interaction bindings. Six continuity invariants and a quiesce-checkpoint-validate-bind-rehydrate-resume protocol govern state preservation, capability changes and continuation authority.

**Key points**

- **Agent boundary.** Identity, memory and unfinished work persist when the body commits them to durable state. The model and harness shape current behavior but are not necessary invariants of longitudinal identity.
- **Implementation.** Enoch implements the design through a reusable software body, private installed state, provider contracts and fenced migration.
- **Operational case.** An established instance keeps its identity, nonempty memory, body revision and historical task IDs across host, joint Codexto-Claude model/harness and chat-surface substitutions.
- **Cross-host task.** A task resumes from a verified artifact checkpoint on a different host.
- **Muse round trips.** A supervised Codex-Muse-Codex study completes five ordinary round trips and five matched same-runtime controls on their first attempts after a separate qualification run.
- **Interruption recovery.** A planned worker interruption recovers through the native task API with the checkpoint preserved, a stale reply rejected and one task completion recorded.
- **Limitations.** The results support mechanical continuity for the tested deployments and bounded workflows. They do not establish behavioral equivalence, arbitrary-task portability, general exactly-once external effects or unattended reliability.
- **Why it matters.** Long-lived agents can move across models, harnesses and servers without silently becoming new agents.
