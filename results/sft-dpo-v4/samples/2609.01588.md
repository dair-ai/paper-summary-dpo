**Curator's take**

Writing needs shift from ideation to revision, and writers differ in what help they want. Existing proactive tools focus on generic text help, like autocomplete, while higher-level support such as idea generation has been reactive. Chao Zhang et al. study proactive thought partners, AI agents that proactively offer customizable, higher-level cognitive support. They built a technology probe that lets users create partners by configuring roles and timing, and deployed it with 16 participants for one week.

**Key points**

- **Customization as planning.** Participants configured partners to match anticipated needs and difficulties, treating configuration as prospective planning.
- **Engagement.** Users often ignored interventions to preserve flow, but engaged suggestions for idea generation and self-monitoring.
- **Lightweight representation.** Participants valued lightweight visual representations and nondirective rhetorical framing for less intrusive interventions.
- **Design implications.** The authors suggest implications around customization, timing, engagement and representation.
