**Curator's take**

Writing involves shifting cognitive activities, and existing proactive tools focus mainly on local text help. Chao Zhang et al. propose “proactive thought partners,” AI agents that offer higher‑level, personalized support at the right moment. They built a technology probe, let 16 users configure partners, and studied how people plan, engage with, and perceive these interventions.

**Key points**

- **Design space.** Proactive thought partners sit in the proactive‑cognitive quadrant, offering higher‑level support without explicit prompting.
- **Customization.** Users create partners by setting roles (type of cognitive support) and timing conditions, treating configuration as prospective planning.
- **Engagement.** Participants often ignored interventions to preserve flow, but used suggestions for idea generation and self‑monitoring when they did engage.
- **Representation.** Lightweight visual cues and nondirective rhetorical framing made interventions feel less intrusive.
- **Why it matters.** The study maps how writers can tailor proactive AI to their shifting needs, informing future writing assistants that go beyond autocomplete.
