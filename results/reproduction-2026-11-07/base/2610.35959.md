**Curator's take**

Long‑horizon agentic tasks usually rely on expensive cloud models, but cheaper efficient models exist. Junlong Li et al. study how to let a capable model hand off to an efficient one during a task, and propose AgentBaton, a lightweight detector that learns when to trigger such handoffs. Their experiments show that a single, well‑timed handoff can cut API costs by about 66% while keeping or even improving accuracy.

**Key points**

- **Handoff direction.** Efficient‑to‑capable handoffs generally underperform capable‑only execution, sometimes at higher cost, whereas capable‑to‑efficient handoffs at suitable stages largely preserve or improve accuracy while reducing cost.
- **Context transfer.** Two methods are compared: in‑context transfer (full history in the prompt) and logfile transfer (structured execution log). The preferred method varies across model pairs, but logfile transfer often benefits efficient models.
- **AgentBaton performance.** Across 11 model pairs and two benchmarks, AgentBaton reduces API costs by 65.9% on average, with accuracy gains of up to 8.8 percentage points over capable‑only execution.
- **Pareto frontier.** AgentBaton extends the empirical accuracy–cost Pareto frontier across reasoning‑effort settings, outperforming single‑model baselines in both cost and accuracy.
