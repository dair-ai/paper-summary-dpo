**Curator's take**

Neural scaling laws assume model size and data affect loss independently, which leads to systematic bias at the edges of the training grid. Mathurin Videau et al. propose the Skaling law, a minimal extension that couples size and data through a single interaction exponent. The new form cuts mean absolute percentage error by 1.5–3× and lets researchers predict full‑grid performance with about ten times less compute.

**Key points**

- **Coupling exponent.** Skaling adds one interaction exponent between model size \(N\) and training tokens \(D\), correcting the boundary bias of the additive Chinchilla law.
- **Error reduction.** Across multiple cross‑validation strategies, Skaling’s MAPE is 1.5–3× lower than Chinchilla’s, with median improvement 2.2× and ≥4× in a third of configurations.
- **Sparse profiling.** An “L‑shape” grid that sweeps data for small models (D‑band) and size for a fixed data amount (N‑band) achieves full‑grid predictive accuracy while cutting profiling compute by ~10×.
- **Why it matters.** Reliable performance prediction from small‑scale experiments lets teams allocate compute budgets more efficiently in next‑generation model training.
