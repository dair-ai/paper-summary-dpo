**Curator's take**

The paper tests whether System One (decision) models are better rerankers than plain reranker fine‑tuning on the same Qwen backbones. The authors compare untuned, decision‑fine‑tuned, and reranker‑fine‑tuned models on TREC DL and BEIR collections and find that decision fine‑tuning never outperforms reranker fine‑tuning.

**Key points**

- **Setup.** Six Qwen backbones from 0.6B to 27B parameters are evaluated on BM25 top‑100 candidates from TREC DL 2019–2023 and five BEIR collections. The untuned model is asked zero‑shot, its decision fine‑tunes (Kev and Clef) and its reranker fine‑tunes (Qwen3‑Reranker, Prism, and two authors’ own models) are compared on ranking and calibration.

- **Ranking.** From 0.6B to 9B, reranker fine‑tuning beats decision fine‑tuning on every backbone by 5 to 29 points nDCG@10. The difference is significant on 48 of 60 reranker‑vs‑collection pairs, never significantly in favour of the decision fine‑tune, and holds on collections the fine‑tune was not trained on. At 27B the two tie. Decision fine‑tuning adds nothing over the untuned model, staying within −2.5 to +0.7 points of it.

- **Calibration.** When asked whether a passage answers the query, decision models from 4B on are better calibrated than the published reranker fine‑tunes. Asked the S1Rank question instead, they lose this advantage while their ranking hardly changes, and the untuned models gain as much from the wording. Calibration does not make up for a worse ranking: up to 9B, a reranker fine‑tune keeps the better passages at a fixed threshold.

- **Why it matters.** If you need a reranker, fine‑tune a reranker rather than a decision model.
