**Curator's take**

Factual completeness in long‑form generation is hard to measure because open‑ended answers cannot be reduced to a flat list of true/false facts. Xilun Chen et al. propose a two‑level meta‑rubric framework that first captures the structure and importance of a complete answer, then mechanically compiles it into a flat binary checklist that an LLM judge can score reliably. They instantiate this as Gamut, a benchmark of 1,813 wearable‑image grounded questions across 10 domains.

**Key points**

- **Meta‑rubric design.** A structured meta‑rubric records the organization, importance, and relations among facts (e.g., critical vs. valuable, mandatory vs. optional). It is mechanically compiled into a flat checklist of binary, machine‑gradable checks that an LLM judge scores.
- **Benchmark scope.** Gamut contains 1,813 questions grounded in real wearable imagery from 10 domains, each paired with an evidence‑backed rubric verified by expert human annotators.
- **Model performance.** Evaluating 14 frontier and open‑weight models, the best score is 58.7% from Gemini 3.1 Pro. Gamut is highly discriminative and robust to the choice of judge.
- **Multimodal vs. text‑only.** Removing visual identification raises scores by a roughly constant amount across models, so the multimodal ranking reflects knowledge completeness rather than perception alone.
- **Why it matters.** Factual recall remains far from solved, and precision‑only methods miss large gaps that Gamut exposes.
