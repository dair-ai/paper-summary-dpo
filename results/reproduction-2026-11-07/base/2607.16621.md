**Curator's take**

Long‑horizon LLM agents need to turn past interactions into executable knowledge. Tang et al. propose MSCE, a training‑free Memory–Skill Co‑Evolution framework that organizes experience into grounded traces, procedural policies, and declarative environmental cognition, and promotes only evidence‑backed, positive‑gain policies into reusable skills.

**Key points**

- **Three‑level memory.** L1 stores grounded step‑level evidence, L2 induces recurring procedural patterns, and L3 abstracts declarative environmental knowledge. This hierarchy separates evidence, procedure, and environmental knowledge.
- **Skill promotion.** A policy becomes a skill only if it retains supporting evidence, shows positive estimated gain, and remains consistent with its trigger, procedure, and applicability boundary. Skills keep evidence anchors, verification rules, and reliability estimates.
- **Reflection‑weighted value back‑filling.** Sparse terminal feedback is propagated through dense local self‑reflections to produce evidence‑calibrated trace values, which guide retrieval, policy induction, environmental cognition, skill promotion, and revision.
- **Results.** Experiments on EvoAgentBench and LoCoMo show that MSCE significantly outperforms state‑of‑the‑art skill‑augmented and memory‑driven baselines, with strong cross‑domain transfer and lifelong‑evolution gains.
