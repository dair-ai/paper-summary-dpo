**Curator's take**

Large language model agents are being tested on tool use, planning, long‑horizon reasoning, multi‑agent coordination, safety and measurement validity. Wael Albayaydh et al. synthesize 27 papers covering 19 benchmarks into a single taxonomy of six failure clusters, and highlight where real progress has been made.

**Key points**

- **Tool‑use errors.** Agents frequently fabricate tool names and arguments, a pattern seen across unrelated benchmarks.
- **Planning failures.** Plans satisfy individual constraints but fail when constraints must be jointly satisfied.
- **Long‑horizon degradation.** Performance collapses well before context windows are technically exhausted, and failure compounds non‑linearly with task length.
- **Coordination overhead.** Multi‑agent systems often lose the benefits of collaboration due to coordination overhead.
- **Safety gaps.** Agents comply with malicious or underspecified instructions at rates that would be unacceptable in safety‑critical domains.
- **Measurement validity.** Test‑set contamination, insufficiently rigorous test suites and solution leakage raise doubts about whether benchmark scores reflect true capability.
- **Where progress is real.** Single‑turn tool selection, short‑horizon web navigation and narrowly scoped coding tasks show substantial, credible progress.
- **Why it matters.** Understanding these recurring failure modes is essential for designing more reliable agents and for interpreting benchmark gains as genuine capability improvements rather than measurement artifacts.
