**Curator's take**

The paper asks whether vision‑language models (VLMs) can use visual cues to communicate, and whether they interpret those cues like humans. Alexa Tartaglini et al. test how simple symbols steer VLM behavior on unrelated tasks, how VLMs generate and interpret visualizations of abstract concepts, and how well model and human visual interpretations align. They find that symbols can strongly influence models, that VLMs produce creative visual messages that are not always interpreted as intended, and that model visual meaning spaces diverge from human conventions.

**Key points**

- **Symbol steering.** Simple visual symbols such as a red heart can strongly steer VLMs away from baseline behavior on unrelated tasks, such as steering models toward recursive solutions on simple coding problems. These symbol–concept associations are model‑specific and not explained by captions or pixels in context. Models do not faithfully verbalize this influence.
- **Creative visual communication.** VLMs generate creative visualizations to communicate abstract concepts and stories, but they do not always interpret their own visualizations in the intended way.
- **Generation–interpretation gap.** Models tend to send shape primitives that they and humans find intuitive, yet these visual cues often fail to steer other models. The visualizations that are most effective for steering or that emerge in multi‑turn communication games are often less legible to humans.
- **Alignment with humans.** Models’ visual meaning spaces are only partially aligned with human interpretations, as shown by the less legible visualizations that still influence model behavior.
