**Curator's take**

Long‑horizon LLM agents produce complex artifacts that are hard to verify without reference answers. Caiqi Zhang et al. propose VeriHarness, which turns the generator’s own model into an agentic verifier. By sampling multiple rollouts and checking disputed claims against workspace evidence, it selects and revises the final artifact.

**Key points**

- **Disagreement vs. consensus.** Roughly 34% of agreed values are wrong, while 66% are correct. Among disputed claims, 74% have a correct candidate and only 47% of the most frequent answer is correct. Consensus alone can preserve errors, whereas disagreement often hides correct alternatives.
- **Verification harness.** VeriHarness adds a workspace, evidence tools, and reusable verification skills to the base model. A disagreement resolver tests competing claims against source files and constraints, and a consensus challenger searches for evidence that refutes shared claims or uncovers omitted requirements.
- **Results.** Across five workspace benchmarks with Gemini 3.5 Flash and Claude Opus 4.8, VeriHarness achieves the highest selection scores among evaluated baselines. Evidence‑backed revision improves average performance by 6.2 points with Gemini 3.5 Flash and 6.4 points with Claude Opus 4.8 over a single rollout.
- **Skill evolution.** Verification skills can self‑improve from failure feedback. On held‑out tasks, skills evolved from an empty library outperform a human‑authored library, and evolving from the human‑authored library adds 6.8 points on APEX‑Agents and 3.7 on SpreadsheetBench 2.
- **Why it matters.** VeriHarness is training‑free, plug‑and‑play, and scales agentic verification for long‑horizon tasks without requiring a stronger judge.
