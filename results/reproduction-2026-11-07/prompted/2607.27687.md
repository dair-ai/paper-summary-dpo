**Curator's take**

Autoresearch improves ML code by proposing changes, training, and keeping improvements. Ji Jiazhen and Ding Shouhong study how the agent’s pre‑execution judgment degrades as successful changes accumulate, a phenomenon they call the confidence cliff. They propose Rehearse, a lightweight skill that proposes several ideas, compares them before execution, and uses focused outcome memory to keep the judgment accurate.

**Key points**

- **Confidence cliff.** On the 366‑pair benchmark, a memoryless judge’s selective accuracy falls from 82.8% to 56.9% once three or more successes have accumulated, while it remains willing to decide.
- **Focused memory.** With focused outcome memory, selective accuracy rises to 83.5%, higher than the 80.9% of the closest‑summary baseline.
- **Coverage.** Without memory, coverage rises from 76% to 85% as successes accumulate, but focused memory lowers coverage while keeping accuracy high.
- **Why it matters.** Late‑stage waste in autoresearch comes from both rarer useful changes and a less reliable judgment; Rehearse targets the judgment component, improving endpoint performance under the same training‑run budget.
