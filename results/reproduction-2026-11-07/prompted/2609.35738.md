Curator's take  
Harness learning tackles the problem of adapting a language‑model agent’s harness—its executable program that organizes model calls and tool use—without changing model parameters. Alvin Zhang et al. train a proposer model to revise harnesses from execution feedback, learning a reusable revision procedure that transfers to unseen tasks.

Key points  

- **Meta‑learning framework.** The proposer is trained via reinforcement learning, with the performance of the revised harness as reward. Only the proposer updates during training; the solver remains frozen.
- **Reasoning Gym results.** On Reasoning Gym, training improves revision quality on task families excluded from both supervised and reinforcement learning. A single‑step trained 4B proposer outperforms the 35B teacher on average.
- **QA transfer.** A proposer trained on HotpotQA transfers to MuSiQue and 2WikiMultihopQA, and can continue improving harnesses over successive test‑time adaptation rounds.
- **Iterative adaptation.** Proposers trained on individual revisions can keep improving harnesses over multiple rounds, and even a proposer trained only on seed‑harness inputs continues to improve revised harnesses.
- **Why it matters.** Harness learning offers a path toward continually learning agents that turn accumulated experience into generalizable improvements without updating model parameters.
