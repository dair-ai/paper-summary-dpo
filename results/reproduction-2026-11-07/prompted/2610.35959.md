Curator's take: Long-horizon agentic tasks often rely on expensive capable models, but cheaper efficient models exist. Junlong Li et al. study how to let a capable model hand off to an efficient one during a task, and propose AgentBaton, a lightweight detector that learns when to hand off based on execution history.

Key points:
- **Handoff direction.** Efficient-to-capable handoffs generally underperform capable-only execution, sometimes at higher cost, even with logfile transfer. Capable-to-efficient handoffs at suitable stages largely preserve or improve accuracy while reducing cost.
- **Context transfer.** Direct in-context transfer or logfile transfer both affect accuracy and cost, with the preferred method varying across model pairs.
- **AgentBaton performance.** Across 11 model pairs and two benchmarks, AgentBaton reduces API costs by 65.9% on average while largely preserving task accuracy, with accuracy gains of up to 8.8 percentage points over capable-only execution.
- **Why it matters.** The study shows that a single, well-timed handoff can make long-horizon agentic tasks more cost-effective without sacrificing accuracy, expanding the empirical accuracy–cost Pareto frontier.
