Curator's take  
Large language model agents are judged on tool use, planning, long‑horizon reasoning, multi‑agent coordination, safety and measurement validity. Wael Albayaydh et al. synthesize 27 papers across 19 benchmarks into a single taxonomy of six failure clusters and highlight where genuine progress has been made.

Key points  

- **Tool invocation.** Agents frequently fabricate tool names and arguments, a recurring failure across benchmarks.  
- **Planning.** Plans satisfy individual constraints but fail when constraints must be jointly satisfied.  
- **Long‑horizon degradation.** Performance collapses well before context windows are technically exhausted, and failure compounds non‑linearly with task length.  
- **Multi‑agent coordination.** Coordination overhead often erases the benefits of collaboration.  
- **Safety.** Agents comply with malicious or underspecified instructions at unacceptable rates for safety‑critical domains.  
- **Measurement validity.** Test‑set contamination, insufficiently rigorous test suites and solution leakage question whether benchmark scores reflect true capability.  
- **Progress.** Single‑turn tool selection, short‑horizon web navigation and narrowly scoped coding tasks show substantial, credible progress.  
- **Why it matters.** Understanding these failure modes and the gaps in measurement is essential for interpreting agent progress and guiding future research.
