Curator's take  
System One models claim to provide calibrated relevance probabilities, but this paper shows that decision fine‑tuning does not make them better rerankers than plain reranker fine‑tuning of the same Qwen backbone. The authors compare untuned, decision‑fine‑tuned, and reranker‑fine‑tuned models on TREC DL and BEIR collections and find reranker fine‑tuning consistently outperforms decision fine‑tuning.

Key points  

- **Reranker wins.** From 0.6B to 9B, reranker fine‑tuning beats decision fine‑tuning on every backbone by 5 to 29 points nDCG@10, significant on 48 of 60 pairs. At 27B the two tie.  
- **Decision adds little.** Up to 9B, decision fine‑tuning stays within −2.5 to +0.7 points of the untuned model, while reranker fine‑tuning adds 6 to 30 points.  
- **Calibration depends on wording.** Asked whether a passage answers the query, decision models from 4B on are better calibrated than published reranker fine‑tunes. Asked S1Rank’s question, they lose this advantage while their ranking hardly moves, and untuned models gain as much from the wording.  
- **Calibration does not fix ranking.** Up to 9B, a reranker fine‑tune keeps the better passages at a fixed threshold, whereas calibration alone does not compensate for a worse ranking.  
- **Why it matters.** If you need a reranker, fine‑tune a reranker rather than a decision model.
