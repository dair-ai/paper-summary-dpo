Curator's take:  
LLM-as-a-Judge models are widely used to evaluate language models, but their validation has relied on exact‑match agreement, which overstates discriminative ability. Justin D. Norman, Michael U. Rivera, and D. Alex Hughes conduct the largest systematic evaluation of 21 judges from nine providers across MTBench, JudgeBench, and RewardBench, and propose a Minimum Viable Validation Protocol that corrects for chance and highlights reliability issues.

Key points:  
**Kappa deflation.** Exact‑match agreement overstates chance‑corrected discrimination by 33–41 percentage points on MT‑Bench across all 21 evaluated models.  
**Ranking instability.** Judge rankings shift by up to 14 positions across benchmarks, showing non‑transferability of performance.  
**Consistency–bias paradox.** Two production‑deployed judges exhibit test–retest reliability > 0.95 while also showing severe position bias > 0.10.  
**Verbosity bias.** All 21 models register a verbosity bias < 0.011 under a single pairwise rubric, far below the 20–40 % variance reported in 2023 literature.  
**JudgeBench sharpness.** JudgeBench discriminates 4.5× more sharply than MT‑Bench, with a κ spread of 60.4 pp versus 13.5 pp.  
**Why it matters.** The study exposes systematic overestimation of judge performance and highlights the need for chance‑corrected metrics and robust validation protocols in LLM evaluation pipelines.
