Curator's take  
Writing assistants often only offer local text help, such as autocomplete. Chao Zhang et al. propose proactive thought partners, AI agents that proactively provide higher‑level, personalized cognitive support during writing. The authors built a technology probe that lets users configure each partner’s role and timing, and studied it with 16 participants over a week.

Key points  

- **Customization.** Participants used the probe to translate anticipated needs into different roles and timing conditions, treating configuration as prospective planning.  
- **Engagement.** Users often ignored interventions to preserve flow, but engaged with suggestions that supported idea generation and self‑monitoring.  
- **Representation.** Proactive support was perceived as less intrusive when delivered through lightweight visual representations and nondirective rhetorical framing.  
- **Why it matters.** The study articulates a design space for proactive writing assistants that go beyond local text help, offering higher‑level cognitive support tailored to individual writers.
