Curator's take  
Factual completeness in long‑form generation is hard to measure because open‑ended questions need richer, structured rubrics, yet judges score more reliably on flat, binary checks. Xilun Chen et al. propose a two‑level meta‑rubric framework that first authoring a structured rubric and then mechanically compiling it into a flat checklist, and instantiate it as Gamut, a benchmark of 1,813 multimodal questions grounded in wearable imagery.

Key points  

- **Meta‑rubric design.** A structured meta‑rubric captures importance, order, and groupings of facts, then compiles into binary, machine‑gradable checks that an LLM judge scores reliably.  
- **Benchmark scope.** Gamut contains 1,813 questions across 10 domains, each paired with evidence‑backed rubrics verified by expert annotators.  
- **Model performance.** Evaluating 14 frontier and open‑weight models, the best score is 58.7% from Gemini 3.1 Pro, showing the benchmark is genuinely challenging.  
- **Robustness.** Gamut is highly discriminative and robust to the choice of judge, and the text‑only variant shows that removing visual identification raises scores by a roughly constant amount across models.  
- **Why it matters.** Factual recall remains far from solved, and precision‑based methods miss large gaps, so a benchmark focused on completeness is needed.
