**Curator's take**

MSCE tackles the problem of turning long-horizon LLM agent experience into reusable, verifiable skills. Bo Tang et al. propose a training-free Memory–Skill Co‑Evolution framework that organizes experience into grounded traces, procedural policies, and declarative environmental cognition, then promotes only evidence-backed, positive-gain policies into callable skills.

**Key points**

- **Memory hierarchy.** MSCE separates experience into L1 trace memory, L2 policy memory, and L3 environmental cognition memory, isolating evidence, procedure, and declarative knowledge.
- **Skill promotion.** A policy becomes a skill only if it retains supporting evidence, shows positive estimated gain, and stays consistent with its trigger, procedure, and applicability boundary.
- **Reflection-weighted value backfilling.** Sparse terminal feedback is propagated through dense local self-reﬂections to produce evidence-calibrated trace values that guide retrieval, policy induction, and skill evolution.
- **Experiments.** On EvoAgentBench and LoCoMo, MSCE significantly outperforms state-of-the-art skill-augmented and memory-driven baselines, with strong cross-domain transferability and lifelong-evolution capabilities.
- **Why it matters.** By converting noisy interaction histories into governable abstractions, MSCE enables LLM agents to reuse experience efficiently and reliably across long-horizon, multi-domain tasks.
