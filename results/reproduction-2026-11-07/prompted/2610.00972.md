Curator's take  
Long‑horizon LLM agents produce complex artifacts that are hard to verify without reference answers. Caiqi Zhang et al. propose VeriHarness, which turns the generator’s own model into an agentic verifier that uses a workspace, evidence tools and reusable verification skills. The verifier resolves disagreements and challenges consensus, then guides artifact selection and revision.

Key points  

**Disagreement reveals correctness.** Roughly 74% of disputed claims contain a correct candidate, while only 47% of the most frequent answers are correct.  

**Consensus can hide errors.** About 34% of agreed values are judged incorrect, so voting alone cannot fix errors.  

**VeriHarness outperforms baselines.** Across five workspace benchmarks with Gemini 3.5 Flash and Claude Opus 4.8, it achieves the highest selection scores among evaluated baselines.  

**Evidence‑backed revision adds points.** Revision improves average performance by 6.2 points with Gemini 3.5 Flash and 6.4 points with Claude Opus 4.8 over a single rollout.  

**Skills can evolve.** Verification skills learned from failure feedback outperform a human‑authored library, adding 6.8 points on APEX‑Agents and 3.7 on SpreadsheetBench 2.  

**Why it matters.** VeriHarness offers a training‑free, plug‑and‑play verifier that scales long‑horizon agentic verification without stronger judges, a critical need as agents take on more complex tasks.
