**Curator's take**

Human‑to‑human interaction is hard to scale, and off‑the‑shelf LLMs used as user simulators distort task difficulty. Hoang Phan et al. propose MIMESIS, a purpose‑built user simulator trained with explicit reasoning supervision and 13 realistic behavioral patterns, and a two‑stage training pipeline that freezes the simulator to train agents with Coached On‑Policy Self‑Distillation.

**Key points**

- **Simulator fidelity.** MIMESIS‑9B scores 65.7 on the SOUL‑Index, surpassing the strongest frontier model. Compared with Claude‑Opus‑5, it improves behavioral fidelity by 13.4 points and reduces Turing distance by 3.6 points on RealUserSim and SimulatorArena.
- **Behavioral realism.** Off‑the‑shelf LLMs raise agent success rates to 82.4–84.4% on τ‑bench, while pretrained Qwen3.5‑4B/9B reduce them by 16.0 and 14.9 points, respectively. Purpose‑built simulators deviate less than 10.3 points from real users, showing better calibration of interaction difficulty.
- **Agent generalization.** Across eight environments, agents trained with MIMESIS outperform those trained with GPT‑5.5 on all nine unseen evaluation user simulators.
- **Coached On‑Policy Self‑Distillation.** CSD uses simulator‑generated private reasoning traces and subsequent utterances to produce dense, token‑level coaching signals, yielding further gains under all nine evaluation users while the deployed agent conditions only on public dialogue.
- **Why it matters.** Accurate, behaviorally realistic user simulators enable scalable training and evaluation of interactive agents, reducing the gap between simulated and real‑user performance.
