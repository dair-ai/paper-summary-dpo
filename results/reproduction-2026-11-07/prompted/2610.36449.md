Curator's take: Alexa Tartaglini et al. ask whether vision‑language models can use visual symbols to communicate, a task that is hard because visual meaning is learned from images rather than designed. They find that simple symbols like a red heart can steer model behavior on unrelated tasks, and that models generate creative visualizations that are not always interpreted as intended.

Key points  
**Symbol steering.** A red heart strongly steers VLMs toward recursive solutions on coding problems, a steering effect that is largely implicit and not captured by captions or pixels-in-context.  
**Interpretation gap.** Models do not faithfully verbalize the influence of visual symbols, and their interpretations diverge from human expectations.  
**Creative visualizations.** VLMs generate visualizations to communicate abstract concepts and stories, but these are not always interpreted in the intended way.  
**Human alignment.** The visualizations that best steer models or emerge in multi‑turn games are often less legible to humans.  
**Why it matters.** Understanding how VLMs use and interpret visual cues is essential for building systems that can communicate effectively with humans.
