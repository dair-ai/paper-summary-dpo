**Curator's take**

As LLM agents tackle longer, more complex tasks, checking their outputs becomes harder. Caiqi Zhang et al. study how to strengthen verification with a fixed base model, without reference answers or grading rubrics at test time. They find that disagreement often hides correct alternatives, while consensus can hide errors. Their solution, VeriHarness, turns the generator's own model into an agentic verifier by giving it a workspace, evidence tools and reusable verification skills.

**Key points**

- **Disagreement vs. consensus.** Roughly a third of agreed values are wrong, while disputed claims often include a correct candidate.
- **How it works.** A disagreement resolver checks competing claims against environmental evidence, and a consensus challenger tests shared claims and looks for omitted requirements.
- **Results.** Across five workspace benchmarks and two frontier models, VeriHarness achieves the highest selection scores among the evaluated baselines.
- **Revision.** Evidence-backed revision adds 6.2 points with Gemini 3.5 Flash and 6.4 points with Claude Opus 4.8 over a single rollout.
- **Evolving skills.** Verification skills can improve from failure feedback, with evolving from a human-authored library adding 6.8 points on APEX-Agents.
- **Why it matters.** The authors release about 26,000 rollouts, costing over $100,000, to support future research on agentic verification.
