**Curator's take**

Reward-maximizing RL collapses to a few high-reward modes, hurting chain-of-thought reasoning. Generative Flow Networks match reward distributions instead, but scaling them to modern LLM post-training is hard. The learned partition function, which normalizes trajectories, adds gradient spikes and engineering overhead. Xiaodong Liu et al. propose GFlowRL, which replaces that network with an in-batch Monte Carlo estimate and adds two stabilizers.

**Key points**

- **Partition function.** The learned partition function adds gradient norms orders of magnitude larger than GRPO, with no visible gains.
- **In-batch estimate.** GFlowRL replaces it with a Monte Carlo estimate from the rollout group, keeping gradient norms at GRPO scale.
- **Stabilizers.** Importance-sampling correction handles rollout/trainer drift, and asymmetric flow-gap clipping handles outlier residuals.
- **Dense 14B.** GFlowRL reaches 2048 Codeforces Elo, 112 above DeepCoder-14B, 144 above FlowRL-14B and 157 above OpenAI's o1.
- **Adversarial red-teaming.** It attains 82.5% ASR@1 on AdvBench and 79.5% on HarmBench, beating the previous multi-turn attacker where FlowRL diverges.
- **MoE.** The recipe transfers to Qwen3-30B-A3B, reaching 1999 Elo with only 3B active parameters, and to Qwen3-235B-A22B on math.
- **Why it matters.** GFlowRL is the first GFlowNet-style RL algorithm to scale stably across both dense and sparse architectures for large reasoning models.
