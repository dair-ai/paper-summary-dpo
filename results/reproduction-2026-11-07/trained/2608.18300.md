**Curator's take**

LLM-as-a-Judge is a common way to scale human evaluation, but most work treats the judge as a static artifact. Emma Yanyang Kong et al. argue that a judge in a deployed system has a lifecycle. They present a four-phase lifecycle for the judges that evaluate recommendation explanations at Netflix, built on controlled online member-facing experiments.

**Key points**

- **Birth.** The pipeline builds curated benchmark datasets with human labels and rationales.
- **Training.** Reasoning-Aligned Rubric Tuning (RART) refines the judge's rubrics, using a meta-judge over reasoning output as the learning signal.
- **Deployment.** One judge serves two online roles: quality gating and reflective generation.
- **Monitoring.** Continuous Human-in-the-Loop alignment detects drift and triggers re-tuning behind a human review gate.
- **Results.** In a five-week A/B test over tens of millions of members on the Netflix mobile app, judge-aligned explanations shifted viewing toward previously unwatched content and increased successful browse-to-play sessions, with no quality-related escalations.
