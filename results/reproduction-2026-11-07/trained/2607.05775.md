**Curator's take**

Large language model agents are being tested on tool use, planning, multi-agent coordination and long horizons, and leaderboards show steady gains. But the papers that introduce and stress-test these benchmarks keep reporting the same failure patterns, from fabricated tool names to plans that fail when constraints must be jointly satisfied. Wael Albayaydh et al. synthesize 27 papers covering 19 benchmarks into a single taxonomy of agent limitations, and they also point out where agents have genuinely improved.

**Key points**

- **Six failure clusters.** Tool invocation errors, planning failures, long-horizon degradation from context accumulation, multi-agent coordination failures, safety failures under adversarial or underspecified conditions, and measurement validity problems.
- **Non-linear failure compounding.** Failure compounds non-linearly with task length, and sub-skill competence does not reliably compose into end-to-end success.
- **Progress where it counts.** Single-turn tool selection, short-horizon web navigation and narrowly scoped coding tasks all show substantial, credible progress.
- **Why it matters.** Progress on agent benchmarks must be distinguished from corrections of earlier measurement error.
