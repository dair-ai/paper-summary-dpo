**Curator's take**

Neural scaling laws predict how loss falls as model size and training data grow, but the standard Chinchilla law assumes the two variables act independently. That assumption makes it under- and overestimate loss at the edges of the training grid. Mathurin Videau et al. propose Skaling, a simple extension that couples model size and data through a single interaction exponent.

**Key points**

- **Coupling matters.** The mixed derivative of loss with respect to model size and data is non-zero across the grid, so scaling them together lowers loss more than scaling either alone. An additive law cannot represent this.
- **MAPE advantage.** Skaling reduces extrapolation MAPE by 1.5 to 3 times relative to Chinchilla across multiple cross-validation strategies.
- **Sparse grids.** When paired with a sparse grid strategy restricted to low-compute regimes, Skaling achieves accurate full-grid extrapolation using about 10 times less compute than uniform sweeps.
- **Why it matters.** Reliable predictions from small-scale experiments let teams allocate compute budgets more robustly and efficiently.
