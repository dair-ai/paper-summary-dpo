**Curator's take**

Long-horizon agentic tasks usually rely on expensive capable models from cloud providers, which can be costly. Cheaper efficient models are emerging, but they are less accurate. Junlong Li et al. ask whether capable and efficient models can collaborate within a single task to cut cost while keeping accuracy. They study model handoffs, where one model takes over from another, and find that efficient-to-capable handoffs underperform capable-only execution. Capable-to-efficient handoffs at suitable stages largely preserve or even improve accuracy while cutting cost. The authors build AgentBaton, a lightweight detector that learns when to hand off from patterns in task progress.

**Key points**

- **Handoff direction.** Efficient-to-capable handoffs generally underperform capable-only execution, sometimes at higher cost.
- **Capable-to-efficient.** These handoffs preserve or improve accuracy while cutting cost, though the best context transfer method varies across models.
- **AgentBaton.** The capable model starts the task. AgentBaton checks progress after each turn and hands off to the efficient model when it predicts a suitable point.
- **Results.** Across 11 model pairs and two benchmarks, AgentBaton cuts API costs by 65.9% on average while largely preserving accuracy, with gains of up to 8.8 percentage points over capable-only execution.
- **Why it matters.** A one-time, well-timed handoff strategy is enough for cost-effective agent collaboration.
