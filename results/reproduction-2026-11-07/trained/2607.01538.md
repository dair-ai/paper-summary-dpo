**Curator's take**

Retrieval is usually done with vector-based methods, but language models could do it by generating the document ID directly from an in-context corpus. Prior work has tested this only on proprietary systems or on small reranking sets, so it is unclear whether LMs can retrieve from large corpora. Siddharth Gollapudi et al. study this at realistic scale, on million-token corpora and on sizes far beyond training. They introduce BLOCK SEARCH, a 0.6B LM retriever, and then add length-aware softmax adjustments and document-level sparse attention to control attention as the corpus grows.

**Key points**

- **BLOCK SEARCH.** It matches a same-backbone dense retriever on small corpora, with 95.8% vs. 95.5% Recall@1 on MS MARCO at about 45k tokens.
- **Attention dilution.** As the corpus grows, irrelevant documents dominate the softmax denominator and the gold document's share falls from 0.91 to 0.01. Individual heads still find the gold document more often than the model decodes it, but that signal also weakens with corpus size.
- **Scaling up.** Length-aware softmax scaling and document-level sparse attention raise Recall@1 on MS MARCO at a million-token context from 0.2% to 20.0%, close to the dense baseline's 20.2%.
- **LIMIT.** On the LIMIT benchmark, the same changes give 13.5% Recall@1 at a million-token corpus, versus 2.9% for the dense baseline.
- **Why it matters.** In-context retrieval could collapse the retriever and generator into one model, but attention control under extreme context growth is a new challenge.
