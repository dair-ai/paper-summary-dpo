**Curator's take**

LLM agents that plan over long horizons often decide by unconstrained generation over a growing log of past actions. As trajectories lengthen, agents can lose track of their objectives, invoke tools out of order and repeat unproductive actions. Yuxing Lu et al. propose the Procedural Graph, an explicit and editable directed graph of procedural knowledge. Nodes abstract tool actions, reasoning steps and states, and edges encode permissible transitions with textual attributes. A guidance model reads the surrounding subgraph at each step and biases the next action without dictating it. An LLM refiner edits the graph after each batch of training tasks, adopting changes that match or improve held-out validation performance.

**Key points**

- **Graph structure.** Nodes are tool actions, reasoning steps and states; edges are annotated with textual attributes that describe how and when the transition should be taken.
- **Guidance.** The guidance model translates the relevant edge attributes into step-level situational guidance that biases the solver's next action.
- **Self-evolution.** An LLM refiner proposes edits to the graph's topology and attributes. A structurally valid candidate graph is adopted if it matches or improves performance on a held-out validation set.
- **Results.** Across multiple datasets, task types and LLMs, the Procedural Graph delivers consistent gains over memory-based baselines. Self-evolution further improves performance without manual engineering.
- **Why it matters.** Procedural knowledge is kept outside the model weights, so it can be inspected, retrieved and edited without retraining.
