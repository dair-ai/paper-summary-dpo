**Curator's take**

LLM-as-a-Judge has become the dominant way to evaluate language models, but judge validation still relies on exact-match agreement, a metric that does not correct for chance and overstates discriminative ability. Justin D. Norman et al. present the largest systematic evaluation of LLM-as-a-Judge to date, covering 21 judges from nine providers across MTBench, JudgeBench and RewardBench. They evaluate under agreement, consistency and bias audit protocols over 118 runs and about 541,000 judgments.

**Key points**

- **Kappa deflation.** Exact match overstates chance-corrected discrimination by 33 to 41 percentage points on MT-Bench in all 21 models.
- **Non-transferable rankings.** Judge rankings shift by up to 14 positions across benchmarks.
- **Consistency-bias paradox.** Two production-deployed judges have test-retest reliability above 0.95 but position bias over 0.10.
- **Low verbosity bias.** All 21 models have verbosity bias under 0.011, against the 20 to 40 percent variance reported in 2023 literature.
- **JudgeBench sharper.** JudgeBench discriminates 4.5 times more sharply than MT-Bench, with a Cohen's kappa spread of 60.4 points versus 13.5 points.
- **Why it matters.** Exact-match agreement overstates judge ability, so judge validation needs a chance-corrected metric.
