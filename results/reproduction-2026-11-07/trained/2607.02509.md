**Curator's take**

Long-context LLMs can read entire documents in one prompt, but they often ignore evidence that is already there. Yanjun Zhao et al. propose ReContext, a training-free inference method that uses the model's own relevance signals to build a query-conditioned evidence pool and replays it before final generation. The full context stays in the prompt, so no pruning or external memory is needed.

**Key points**

- **Sparse evidence.** In a 128K-token context, the top 0.1% of tokens already cover about 50% to 80% of accumulated relevance.
- **Recursive selection.** Each round of evidence selection conditions on the original context, the question and the evidence pool built so far.
- **Results.** On eight 128K datasets with Qwen3-4B, Qwen3-8B and Llama3-8B, ReContext improves mean accuracy over Vanilla from 0.24 to 0.30, a 24.6% relative gain, and has the best average rank on all three backbones.
- **Why it matters.** ReContext improves evidence use without training, external memory or context pruning.
