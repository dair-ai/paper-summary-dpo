**Curator's take**

Agent skills are reusable instructions stored as documents, and they let LLM agents solve tasks beyond their pretraining. Building a comprehensive skill library is hard and expensive, so agents could instead learn skills from the task descriptions and feedback they receive. Tianyi Guan et al. introduce ContinualSkillBench, a benchmark that tests whether agents can evolve skills from experience and whether those skills improve performance.

**Key points**

- **Five domains.** Each domain has 100 interconnected subtasks anchored to three core skills. The tasks are ordered by difficulty and skill dependencies.
- **Sequential execution helps.** Across 14 of 15 model-domain combinations, running tasks in sequence improves performance.
- **Skill maintenance vs. context.** Across two models and three domains, keeping context and feedback without a skill library gives nearly the same mean performance as maintaining explicit skills.
- **When explicit skills help.** Explicit skills are better for tasks that need reusable procedures or precise outputs. Full-context ICL is often stronger on open-ended tasks.
- **Skill quality matters.** Weaker agents accumulate more skills but reuse them less often and produce lower-quality repositories.
- **Why it matters.** Current agents can adapt through sequential interaction but still struggle to consolidate experience into transferable skills.
