**Curator's take**

Vision-language models are trained on billions of images, but do they share our ability to communicate visually? Alexa Tartaglini et al. test whether simple visual symbols steer VLM behavior on unrelated tasks, how VLMs use visuals to communicate abstract concepts, and how their visual meanings line up with human conventions. They find that VLMs can be steered by a red heart, but the steering is largely implicit and their visual cues often fail to steer other models.

**Key points**

- **Steering by symbols.** A red heart can steer VLMs toward recursive solutions on simple coding problems. The steering is model-specific and not explained by captions or pixels-in-context.
- **Generation-interpretation gap.** Models send shape primitives that humans find intuitive, but these cues often fail to steer other models.
- **Creative communication.** In games about abstract concepts and stories, models can use visuals to transmit meaning, though some communications remain non-intuitive to humans.
- **Partial human alignment.** The visuals that best steer or emerge in multi-turn games are often less legible to humans.
- **Why it matters.** VLMs can communicate visually, but their visual meaning diverges from human conventions in surprising ways.
