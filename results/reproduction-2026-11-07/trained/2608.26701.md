**Curator's take**

Co-Scientist is a Gemini-based multi-agent system that moves beyond in silico hypothesis generation. Samuel Schmidgall et al. extend it into an execution-grounded research partner that designs experiments, runs them, and writes papers. The authors validate the system across materials science, biology and computer science, producing real-world results.

**Key points**

- **Materials science.** Co-Scientist designed a non-hazardous precursor route for Ti3 C2 T_x MXene growth. The top-ranked recipe, executed by human experts, yielded 2D layered structures with key structural similarities to Ti3 C2 T_x MXene, though further experiments are needed to confirm the atomic structure. The system also grew monolayer MoS2, MoSe2 and WS2 in single attempts, tailoring recipes to laboratory constraints in minutes.
- **Biology.** Co-Scientist built a system to predict emergent swarming phenotypes of engineered E. coli across IPTG concentration gradients from sparse imaging data. The predictions largely matched unpublished wet-lab morphological measurements, suggesting a potential to reduce experimental screening cycles.
- **Computer science.** The system autonomously designed an inference-time scaling architecture that outperformed six frontier models on HealthBench Hard and Professional while reducing potential clinical harm under blinded physician evaluation.
- **Paper writing.** A double-blind study with 30 domain experts across 450 reviews found that Co-Scientist's reliability modules reduce hallucination and plagiarism compared to unconstrained baselines.
- **Why it matters.** The work shows a generalizable research framework that orchestrates discovery across diverse experimental surfaces while keeping experimental verifiability throughout the workflow.
