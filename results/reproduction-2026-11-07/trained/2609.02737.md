**Curator's take**

Language models spend most of their attention on a small fraction of context, yet they read the entire KV cache to find the few tokens that matter. A prominent approach pre-selects relevant tokens via lightweight proxy scores, but this still costs O(N) per step. Namgyu Ho et al. propose Declarative Attention (DA), a protocol that lets the model declare where it will attend. The inference engine parses these declarations like tool calls and skips most of the KV cache read.

**Key points**

- **Three modes.** DA partitions generation into <global> (full context), <focus> (a specific region) and <local> (recent output only). Mode transitions are emitted as parseable tokens in the chain-of-thought.
- **Zero-shot results.** Across 15 long-context tasks, DA reduces total attended tokens per response by 45.8% on Gemma-4-31B and 19.6% on Qwen-3.6-27B, with accuracy drops of 1.40pp and 2.10pp.
- **Scaling.** The accuracy gap shrinks as model size grows from 4B to 31B, and the cost reduction grows with context length, saving up to 19M tokens per response.
- **Wall time.** Integrated into vLLM, DA cuts per-step decode time by up to 55%.
- **Why it matters.** Models can already know which parts of context matter, so DA removes the per-step selection cost of proxy scores.
