**Curator's take**

A language-model agent is defined by its model and its harness, the program that organizes model calls and tool use. Adapting the harness can improve performance without changing model parameters, but good revisions often require repeated testing and may not transfer to other tasks. Alvin Zhang et al. introduce harness learning, which trains a proposer model to revise a harness from execution feedback. The proposer edits executable harness code, and at test time it refines the harness without updating any parameters.

**Key points**

- **Training.** The proposer is trained with reinforcement learning, using the performance of the revised harness as the reward. It can also be initialized with supervised fine-tuning.
- **Results on Reasoning Gym.** Training improves revision quality on task families excluded from both supervised and reinforcement learning. In single-step revision, the trained 4B proposer outperforms the 35B teacher on average.
- **Transfer.** A proposer trained on HotpotQA transfers to MuSiQue and 2WikiMultihopQA.
- **Iterative adaptation.** Proposers trained on individual revisions can continue improving harnesses over successive rounds of test-time adaptation. Even a proposer trained only on seed-harness inputs keeps improving revised harnesses.
- **Why it matters.** Harness learning lets agents turn execution experience into generalizable improvements without updating model parameters.
