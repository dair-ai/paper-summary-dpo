**Curator's take**

Long-horizon problem solving and scientific research need computation to accumulate across attempts. Most inference-time work is organized around individual trajectories, so partial solutions or failed experiments can disappear when an episode ends. Vedant Shah et al. propose compounding inference, where intermediate work persists and can be inspected, extended, combined or challenged later. Their system, GitSwarm, uses a shared, branchable Git repository as structured persistent memory. Homogeneous agents act asynchronously, decide what to do with previous contributions and publish each contribution as an atomic commit. Semantic dependencies record how later work builds on work across branches.

**Key points**

- **All 30 IMO problems.** On IMOProofBench-Advanced, GitSwarm solves all 30 problems in one run using GPT-5.5.
- **ProgramBench.** With GPT-5.5 workers, it scores 79.4% mean, compared with 65.1% for the strongest reported baseline under the same budget.
- **Neural architecture research.** On Residual Matrix Transformer, Looped Transformer and NanoChat, GitSwarm improves upon the starting architectures through successive experimentation.
- **Accumulation.** On ProgramBench, 94.7% of contributions are subsequently built upon, and the selected solution's ancestry covers 82-93% of the contribution graph.
- **Why it matters.** Persistent memory lets inference-time computation accumulate across otherwise independent episodes, forming an evolving body of work that later inference can reuse.
