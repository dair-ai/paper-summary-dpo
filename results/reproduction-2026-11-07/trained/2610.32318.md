**Curator's take**

System One models, or decision models, give a calibrated probability instead of generated text. Within weeks of the first release, Jev was used as a reranker. Dinzinger et al. compare decision fine-tuning with plain reranker fine-tuning on the same backbones. They find that decision fine-tuning never makes the better reranker.

**Key points**

- **Setup.** Six Qwen backbones from 0.6B to 27B parameters rerank the same BM25 candidates from TREC DL 2019-2023 and five BEIR collections. The untuned model is asked zero-shot, its decision fine-tunes (Kev and Clef) and its reranker fine-tunes are compared on ranking and calibration.
- **Ranking.** From 0.6B to 9B, reranker fine-tuning wins on every backbone by 5 to 29 points nDCG@10. The difference is significant on 48 of the 60 pairs of reranker fine-tune and collection. At 27B the two tie. Decision fine-tuning adds nothing over the untuned model up to 9B, staying within -2.5 to +0.7 points of simply asking the untuned model.
- **Calibration.** Asked whether a passage answers the query, decision models from 4B on are better calibrated than the published reranker fine-tunes. Asked a slightly different question, they lose this advantage.
- **Why it matters.** If you need a reranker, fine-tune a reranker.
