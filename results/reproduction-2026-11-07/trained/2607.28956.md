**Curator's take**

LLM agents are usually tested on bounded tasks with clear success criteria, but real deployments often need long-term coherence: keeping purposeful behavior over extended horizons while adapting to evidence that arrives at different times. Seller-side e-commerce fits this test, with decisions over sourcing, listing, pricing, cash flow and feedback adaptation that recur over time. Qiming Shi et al. introduce MerchantBench, a 365-day simulation grounded in 98,843 real product records and 26 tools for agent interaction. Upstream supplier events are observable promptly, while downstream order outcomes arrive with delay, so agents must follow individual order lifecycles and revisit earlier decisions.

**Key points**

- **Persistent environment.** The simulator updates demand, supplier states, order lifecycles, cash flow, penalties and store reputation over the year.
- **Partial observability.** Upstream supplier events and downstream order outcomes become visible at different times.
- **Results.** Across 48 runs of 365 simulated days, eight LLMs under two agent frameworks are evaluated. The best configuration reaches only 27.3% of the mean final net assets achieved by human participants.
- **Why it matters.** The gap shows that even the latest LLMs fall far short of humans on long-term coherence.
