**Curator's take**

Writing involves shifting cognitive activities, from ideation to revision, and writers' needs vary across individuals and moments. Existing proactive tools focus on generic textual assistance, like autocomplete, while higher-level support has largely been reactive. Chao Zhang et al. study proactive thought partners, AI agents that proactively offer customizable, higher-level cognitive support during writing. They instantiate the idea in a technology probe and deploy it with 16 participants for one week.

**Key points**

- **Customization as planning.** Participants configured partners by defining roles and timing conditions, treating configuration as prospective planning for anticipated needs.
- **Engagement.** Users often ignored interventions to preserve flow, but engaged suggestions supported idea generation and self-monitoring.
- **Lightweight representation.** Participants valued lightweight visual representations and nondirective rhetorical framing for less intrusive interventions.
- **Design implications.** The authors suggest implications around customization, timing, engagement and representation.
