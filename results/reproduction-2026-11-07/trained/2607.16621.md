**Curator's take**

Long-horizon LLM agents need memory to keep coherence and reuse experience, but existing systems store traces as passive context. Repeated traces still get re-read, wasting tokens. Turning raw traces into reusable skills is hard because traces contain failures and artifacts, and sparse terminal feedback makes credit assignment uncertain. Bo Tang et al. propose MSCE, a training-free Memory-Skill Co-Evolution framework. It organizes experience into grounded step traces, reusable procedural policies and declarative environmental cognition, and promotes only supported, positive-gain policies into callable skills.

**Key points**

- **Three memory levels.** L1 trace memory stores grounded step evidence. L2 policy memory induces recurring procedural patterns. L3 environmental cognition memory abstracts declarative knowledge about structure and constraints.
- **Governed promotion.** A policy becomes a skill only if it keeps supporting evidence, shows positive estimated gain and stays consistent with its trigger and applicability boundary.
- **Reflection-weighted value backfilling.** Sparse terminal feedback propagates through dense self-references to give evidence-calibrated trace values for retrieval, policy induction and skill promotion.
- **Results.** On EvoAgentBench and LoCoMo, MSCE significantly outperforms state-of-the-art skill-augmented and memory-driven baselines, with strong cross-domain transfer and lifelong-evolution gains.
- **Why it matters.** Skills that keep evidence links and reliability estimates are less likely to be invoked in inappropriate contexts, saving tokens and improving long-horizon performance.
