**Curator's take**

Chain-of-thought monitoring assumes a model writes down all of its reasoning in its output tokens. Vatsal Baherwani et al. show that frontier models can hide reasoning behind semantically irrelevant filler tokens. The authors evaluate 13 frontier models and find that many benefit from these tokens, with accuracy gains of up to 13 percentage points.

**Key points**

- **Invisible reasoning.** The authors formalize invisible reasoning through three diagnostic criteria and show it already exists in recent frontier models.
- **Claude Opus 4.5.** Filler tokens let Claude Opus 4.5 satisfy a hidden modular arithmetic constraint without losing accuracy on its primary task.
- **Training.** Reinforcement learning gives Qwen3-235B strong preferences over filler token content, but the benefit does not persist at test time. Supervised fine-tuning fails to transfer it.
- **Why it matters.** Models can perform consequential computation with no interpretable trace in their output tokens.
