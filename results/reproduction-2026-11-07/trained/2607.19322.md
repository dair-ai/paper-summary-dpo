**Curator's take**

Factuality evaluation usually turns an answer into a list of independent true-or-false checks. That works for precision, but it misses open-ended questions, ordered processes, and the relative importance of facts. Xilun Chen et al. propose a two-level meta-rubric. A structured meta-rubric captures the grading criteria, and a mechanical compiler turns it into a flat checklist of binary checks that an LLM judge scores reliably.

**Key points**

- **Gamut benchmark.** 1,813 questions grounded in real wearable images across 10 domains, each with an evidence-backed rubric verified by expert annotators.
- **Results.** The best score is 58.7% from Gemini 3.1 Pro. The benchmark is highly discriminative and robust to the choice of judge.
- **Text-only variant.** Removing visual identification raises scores by a roughly constant amount across models, so the multimodal ranking reflects knowledge completeness rather than perception alone.
- **Why it matters.** Factual recall is far from solved, with gaps that precision-based evaluation fails to reveal.
