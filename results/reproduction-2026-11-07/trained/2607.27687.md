**Curator's take**

Autoresearch improves code by proposing changes, running full training jobs and keeping the ones that improve the metric. The loop's efficiency depends on deciding before a training run whether a modification will work. Ji Jiazhen et al. study how that pre-execution judgment changes as an autoresearch trajectory accumulates successful changes. They call the drop in accuracy while coverage stays high the "confidence cliff" and propose Rehearse, a lightweight skill that proposes several ideas, compares them before execution and remembers only the most similar past outcomes.

**Key points**

- **Helpfulness falls.** In public AutoSOTA logs, helpful modifications fall from 70% in the first two iterations to 43% by iteration 6+.
- **Judge accuracy.** On 296 same-baseline pairs, an LLM judge with candidate rationales but no prior history reaches 79.5% accuracy where strict consensus returns a verdict.
- **Confidence cliff.** On the full 366-pair benchmark, selective accuracy falls from 82.8% to 56.9% once three or more successes have accumulated, while the judge remains willing to decide.
- **Focused memory helps.** With focused outcome memory, selective accuracy rises to 83.5%.
- **Rehearse improves endpoints.** Across 4,000 budgeted training runs over three loops, Rehearse improves the endpoint under the same training-run budget on nanochat, image classification and time-series forecasting.
- **Why it matters.** As useful changes become rarer, the agent also becomes less reliable at judging which remaining changes are worth a run. Rehearse targets that judgment.
