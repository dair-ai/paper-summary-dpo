Curator's take:  
The paper tackles the problem that LLM-as-a-Judge evaluation relies on exact‑match agreement, which does not correct for chance and inflates perceived discriminative power. Justin D. Norman, Michael U. Rivera, and D. Alex Hughes conduct the largest systematic, cross‑benchmark study of judge reliability, showing that raw agreement overstates true agreement by 33–41 percentage points and that judge rankings are not transferable across benchmarks. They propose a Minimum Viable Validation Protocol to address these issues.

Key points:  
**Kappa deflation.** Raw agreement overstates chance‑corrected discrimination by 33–41 percentage points on MT‑Bench across all 21 evaluated models.  

**Ranking instability.** Judge rankings shift by up to 14 positions across benchmarks, indicating non‑transferability of performance.  

**Consistency–bias paradox.** Two production‑deployed judges exhibit high test–retest reliability (> 0.95) while also showing severe position bias (> 0.10).  

**Verbosity bias.** All 21 models register a verbosity bias of less than 0.011 under a single pairwise rubric, far below the 20–40 % variance reported in 2023 literature.  

**JudgeBench sharpness.** JudgeBench discriminates 4.5× more sharply than MT‑Bench, with a Cohen’s κ spread of 60.4 pp versus 13.5 pp.  

**Why it matters.** The study reveals that common validation metrics inflate judge performance, undermining trust in LLM‑based evaluation pipelines and highlighting the need for chance‑corrected, multi‑protocol validation standards.
