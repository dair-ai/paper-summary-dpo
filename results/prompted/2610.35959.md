Curator's take:  
Long‑horizon agentic tasks often rely on expensive cloud models, but cheaper efficient models exist.  
Li et al. study how to let a capable model hand off to an efficient one during a task, and they propose AgentBaton, a lightweight detector that learns when to trigger such handoffs.  
The detector enables cost savings while keeping accuracy high.

Key points  
**Handoff direction.** Efficient‑to‑capable handoffs generally underperform capable‑only execution and offer little or no cost reduction, even when using logfile transfer.  
**Capable‑to‑efficient advantage.** Capable‑to‑efficient handoffs at suitable stages largely preserve or improve accuracy while substantially reducing cost; the best context‑transfer method varies across models.  
**AgentBaton performance.** Across 11 model pairs and two benchmarks, AgentBaton reduces API costs by 65.9 % on average while largely preserving task accuracy, with accuracy gains of up to 8.8 percentage points over capable‑only execution.  
**Why it matters.** The work shows that a single, well‑timed handoff strategy can extend the empirical accuracy–cost Pareto frontier for long‑horizon agentic tasks, making powerful cloud models more cost‑effective.
