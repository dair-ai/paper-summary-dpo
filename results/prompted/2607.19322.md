Curator's take:  
The paper tackles the problem of measuring factual completeness in open‑ended, long‑form generation. First author Xilun Chen et al. propose a two‑level meta‑rubric framework that first encodes rich, structured evaluation criteria and then mechanically compiles them into flat, binary checks that an LLM judge can score reliably. They instantiate this framework as Gamut, a benchmark of 1,813 multimodal questions grounded in wearable imagery.

Key points  
**Meta‑rubric design.** A structured meta‑rubric captures the organization, importance, and relations among facts, then compiles into a flat checklist of binary, machine‑gradable checks that an LLM judge scores reliably.  

**Benchmark scope.** Gamut contains 1,813 questions across 10 domains, each paired with an evidence‑backed rubric verified by expert human annotators.  

**Evaluation results.** Evaluating 14 frontier and open‑weight models, the best score is 58.7 % from Gemini 3.1 Pro, showing the benchmark is genuinely challenging and highly discriminative.  

**Robustness to judge choice.** Rankings align closely with independently understood model capabilities and are robust to the choice of judge.  

**Why it matters.** The work highlights factual recall as an understudied target and shows that precision‑based evaluation fails to reveal large gaps in knowledge completeness.
