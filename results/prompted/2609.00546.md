**Curator's take**  
Zhenyu Zhao and Roy Zhao tackle the problem of keeping an AI agent’s identity, memory, and executable body intact when its model, harness, or host changes. They propose a runtime‑independent architecture that separates a continuity‑bearing substrate (I, M, B) from replaceable execution and interaction bindings, and they implement it in the Enoch system.

**Key points**

- **Agent boundary.** The authors define a persistent substrate (I, M, B) that carries identity, memory, and executable body, while reasoner, harness, host, and interaction surfaces are replaceable bindings.

- **Migration protocol.** A quiesce–checkpoint–validate–bind–rehydrate–resume protocol enforces six continuity invariants and explicit failure semantics for authorized migration.

- **Operational evidence.** An operational case shows that an instance’s identity, nonempty memory, body revision, and historical task IDs survive host, Codexto‑Claude model/harness, and chat‑surface substitutions.

- **Cross‑host task resume.** A separate cross‑host task resumes from a verified artifact checkpoint, demonstrating continuity across deployments.

- **Supervised round‑trip study.** A Codex–Muse–Codex study completes five ordinary round trips and five matched same‑runtime controls on first attempts after a separate qualification run, showing that the system can maintain continuity in a supervised setting.

- **Worker interruption recovery.** A planned worker interruption recovers through the native task API with the checkpoint preserved, a stale reply rejected, and one task completion recorded, illustrating robustness to interruptions.

- **Why it matters.** The work provides a concrete architecture and protocol for preserving agent continuity across models, harnesses, and servers, addressing a key lifecycle challenge for long‑lived AI agents.
