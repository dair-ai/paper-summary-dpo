Curator's take:  
System One models, or decision models, promise calibrated relevance probabilities for reranking. First‑author Michael Dinzinger et al. compare decision fine‑tuning with plain reranker fine‑tuning on six Qwen backbones (0.6B–27B) over TREC DL and BEIR collections. They find that decision fine‑tuning never outperforms a reranker fine‑tune, and that calibration only helps when the question is “does the passage answer the query”.

Key points  
**Decision fine‑tune vs. reranker fine‑tune.** From 0.6B to 9B, reranker fine‑tuning beats decision fine‑tuning on every backbone by 5 to 29 points nDCG@10; the difference is significant on 48 of 60 pairs. At 27B the two tie.  
**Untuned model baseline.** Up to 9B, decision fine‑tuning adds nothing over the untuned model, staying within −2.5 to +0.7 points of simply asking the untuned model the same question, while reranker fine‑tuning adds 6 to 30 points.  
**Calibration depends on wording.** When asked “does the passage answer the query”, decision models from 4B on are better calibrated than published reranker fine‑tunes; when asked the looser S1Rank question, they lose this advantage and the untuned models gain as much from the wording.  
**Calibration does not compensate for ranking.** Up to 9B, a reranker fine‑tune keeps the better passages at a fixed threshold, whereas decision fine‑tuning’s calibration advantage does not improve ranking.  
**Why it matters.** The study shows that, contrary to recent reports, System One models are not superior rerankers; fine‑tuning a dedicated reranker remains the recommended approach for retrieval systems.
