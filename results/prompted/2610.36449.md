Curator's take:  
Alexa Tartaglini et al. investigate whether vision‑language models can use visual symbols to influence behavior and communicate ideas. They find that simple shapes and colors can steer models on unrelated tasks, yet the models’ verbal explanations do not reflect this influence. The work also shows that VLMs can generate creative visualizations for abstract concepts, but these visuals are often misinterpreted by the models themselves and are less legible to humans.

Key points:  
**Symbol steering.** Simple visual symbols such as a red heart can strongly steer VLM behavior on unrelated tasks, but the models do not faithfully verbalize this influence.  
**Creative visualizations.** VLMs generate creative visualizations to communicate abstract concepts and stories, yet they do not always interpret their own visualizations as intended.  
**Human alignment.** The visualizations that are most effective for steering or that emerge in multi‑turn communication games are often less legible to humans, indicating a partial misalignment between model and human visual interpretation.  
**Why it matters.** Understanding how VLMs process and use visual cues is essential for developing reliable visual communication interfaces between humans and AI.
