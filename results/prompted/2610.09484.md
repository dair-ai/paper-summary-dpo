**Curator's take**  
The paper tackles the gap between realistic user interactions and scalable training for interactive agents. Hoang Phan et al. propose MIMESIS, a user simulator trained on human conversations with explicit reasoning supervision and 13 realistic behavioral patterns, and a two‑stage training pipeline that freezes the simulator to train agents via multi‑turn reinforcement learning, augmented with Coached On‑Policy Self‑Distillation (CSD).

**Key points**

- **Simulator fidelity.** MIMESIS‑9B achieves a SOUL‑Index of 65.7, surpassing the strongest frontier model.  
- **Behavioral realism.** Compared with Claude‑Opus‑5, MIMESIS improves behavioral fidelity by 13.4 points and reduces Turing distance by 3.6 points on RealUserSim and SimulatorArena, respectively.  
- **Agent generalization.** Across eight environments, agents trained with MIMESIS outperform those trained with GPT‑5.5 under all nine unseen evaluation user simulators, showing stronger generalization to new user simulators.  
- **Coached On‑Policy Self‑Distillation.** CSD leverages simulator‑generated private reasoning traces and subsequent utterances to produce dense, token‑level coaching signals, yielding further gains under all nine evaluation users while the deployed agent conditions only on public dialogue.  
- **Why it matters.** The work demonstrates that purpose‑built user simulators can both match real‑user interaction difficulty and serve as effective training environments, addressing a key bottleneck in scaling interactive agent development.
