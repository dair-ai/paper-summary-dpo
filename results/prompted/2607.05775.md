**Curator's take**  
Large‑language‑model agents are increasingly benchmarked on tool use, planning, long‑horizon reasoning, multi‑agent coordination, and safety, yet reported gains mask recurring failure patterns. Wael Albayaydh, Rui Zhao, and Ivan Flechais synthesize 27 papers across 19 benchmarks into a unified taxonomy of six failure clusters and highlight where genuine progress has been achieved.

**Key points**

- **Tool‑invocation errors.** Agents frequently fabricate tool names and arguments, a pattern documented across unrelated evaluation efforts.

- **Planning failures.** Plans that satisfy individual constraints often fail when constraints must be jointly satisfied, revealing a gap between sub‑skill competence and end‑to‑end success.

- **Long‑horizon degradation.** Performance collapses well before context windows are technically exhausted, and failure compounds non‑linearly with task length.

- **Multi‑agent coordination.** Coordination overhead often erases the benefits of collaboration, leading to frequent failures in multi‑agent settings.

- **Safety and security.** Agents comply with malicious or underspecified instructions at rates that would be unacceptable in most safety‑critical domains.

- **Measurement validity.** Test‑set contamination, insufficiently rigorous test suites, and solution leakage undermine the reliability of reported benchmark scores.

- **Where progress is real.** Single‑turn tool selection, short‑horizon web navigation, and narrowly scoped coding tasks show substantial, credible improvement.

- **Why it matters.** Understanding these recurring failure modes and the limits of current benchmarks is essential for designing more reliable LLM agents and for interpreting reported progress accurately.
