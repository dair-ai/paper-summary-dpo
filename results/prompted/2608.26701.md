**Curator's take**  
Co‑Scientist tackles the gap between AI‑generated hypotheses and physical validation by extending a Gemini‑based multi‑agent system into an execution‑grounded research partner. The authors demonstrate that, with human‑in‑the‑loop oversight, the system can design safe chemical vapor deposition protocols, predict bacterial swarming phenotypes, and autonomously discover a superior inference‑time scaling architecture, while also reducing hallucination and plagiarism in end‑to‑end manuscript generation.

**Key points**

- **Materials synthesis.** Co‑Scientist interfaced with a semi‑automated CVD reactor to design a non‑hazardous precursor route (C₂Cl₆) for Ti₃C₂Tₓ MXene growth. Physical execution of the top‑ranked recipe yielded 2D layered structures exhibiting key structural similarities to Ti₃C₂Tₓ MXene, though further experiments are needed to confirm the atomic structure. The system also achieved single‑attempt growth of monolayer MoS₂, MoSe₂, and WS₂ semiconductors by tailoring synthesis recipes to laboratory constraints in minutes.

- **Biology prediction.** With domain experts refining the task framing, Co‑Scientist built a vision pipeline that predicts emergent swarming phenotypes of engineered E. coli across IPTG concentration gradients (0–10 mM) from sparse imaging data. The predictions quantitatively matched unpublished wet‑lab morphological measurements, suggesting a potential for reducing experimental screening cycles.

- **Computer science architecture.** Operating fully autonomously, Co‑Scientist discovered an inference‑time scaling architecture that outperformed six frontier models on HealthBench Hard and Professional, while achieving a modest reduction in potential clinical harm under blinded physician evaluation.

- **Scientific integrity.** A double‑blind study with 30 domain experts and 450 independent reviews showed that Co‑Scientist’s log‑based verification and safety mechanisms consistently reduce severe hallucination and plagiarism compared to unconstrained baseline systems.

- **Why it matters.** The work demonstrates a generalizable research framework that orchestrates discovery across diverse experimental surfaces—materials synthesis, wet‑lab assays, and computational code—while maintaining experimental verifiability throughout the scientific workflow, thereby advancing the feasibility of closed‑loop, AI‑accelerated scientific discovery.
