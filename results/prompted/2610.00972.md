Curator's take:  
VeriHarness tackles the problem of verifying long‑horizon agent outputs when no stronger judge or reference answers are available. Caiqi Zhang et al. propose turning the generator’s own model into an agentic verifier that uses a workspace, evidence tools, and reusable verification skills to resolve disagreements and challenge consensus. The harness then guides artifact selection and revision, improving performance over single‑rollout baselines.

Key points  
**Disagreement reveals correct alternatives.** Roughly 74 % of disputed claims contain a correct candidate, while only 47 % of the most frequent answers are correct.  
**Consensus can hide errors.** About 34 % of agreed values are judged incorrect, showing that majority voting alone is insufficient.  
**VeriHarness outperforms baselines.** Across five workspace benchmarks and two frontier models, VeriHarness achieves the highest selection scores among evaluated baselines.  
**Evidence‑backed revision improves results.** Revision guided by verification findings yields gains of 6.2 points with Gemini 3.5 Flash and 6.4 points with Claude Opus 4.8 over a single rollout.  
**Skills can self‑improve.** Verification skills evolved from an empty library outperform a human‑authored library, and further evolution adds 6.8 points on APEX‑Agents and 3.7 on SpreadsheetBench 2.  
**Why it matters.** VeriHarness offers a training‑free, plug‑and‑play verification framework that scales with frontier models, enabling more reliable long‑horizon agentic tasks without requiring stronger external judges.
