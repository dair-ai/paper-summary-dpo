**Curator's take**  
Autoresearch agents improve ML code by proposing changes, training, and keeping improvements. Ji Jiazhen and Ding Shouhong study how the agent’s pre‑execution judgment degrades as more successful changes accumulate, a phenomenon they call the confidence cliff, and propose Rehearse, a lightweight skill that adds a pre‑run comparison step and focused outcome memory to keep the judgment reliable.

**Key points**

- **Confidence cliff.** On the 366‑pair benchmark, a memoryless judge’s selective accuracy drops from 82.8 % at cold start to 56.9 % once three or more successes have accumulated, while its willingness to decide remains high.

- **Focused outcome memory.** Using a focused store of similar past outcomes, Rehearse raises late selective accuracy to 83.5 %, compared with 80.9 % for the closest‑summary baseline.

- **Pre‑run comparison.** The Rehearse skill proposes several ideas, compares them before execution, and runs only the most promising, thereby reducing wasted training runs.

- **Why it matters.** By stabilizing pre‑execution judgment, Rehearse improves the endpoint under the same training‑run budget on nanochat, image classification, and time‑series forecasting.
