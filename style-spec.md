# Style spec for preferred summaries

Derived from Elvis's 10 calibration picks and reasons on Nov 2, then revised after his notes on the first round of edits. An edit may select, reorder and tighten facts. It never adds a fact that is not in the paper's title, abstract or introduction.

## Content
1. The curator's take names the problem and what the authors propose. It contains no results. Every result goes in the key points, with its benchmark and comparison.
2. Key points carry the most useful findings. Each number keeps its benchmark and comparison.
3. Keep methodology details when the input gives them. Cut only bullets with no specific content, such as an "Ablations" bullet that names no finding.
4. Keep caveats the authors state.

## Style
5. No em dashes or en dashes.
6. Credit the work as "First author et al." (for example, "Gaotang Li et al. propose"). Name both authors when there are two, and the one author when there is one. No long author or institution lists.
7. No references to the paper's own sections, figures, tables or definitions.
8. Concise, direct sentences. The curator's take reads as one connected line of reasoning: the problem, why it is hard or why current approaches fall short, then what the authors propose and how it works. Each sentence follows from the one before.
9. Key points use a short, specific bold label ending in a period, then one or two sentences.
10. Every summary ends with a "Why it matters" key point, written from what the input says about the significance of the work.
11. Keep each distinct finding from the base summary as its own key point when it carries a specific fact, such as coverage figures.
